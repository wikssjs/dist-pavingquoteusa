What thickness should an asphalt driveway be in Pennsylvania?
For a residential driveway in St. Thomas, the standard is 2-3 inches of compacted hot-mix asphalt over a 4-6 inch compacted gravel base. Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors working for homeowners generally need to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office when the job exceeds $500. You can verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be cautious if someone tries to solicit driveway work door-to-door after a storm (“storm chasers”) without a verifiable PA HIC registration—this is one of the fastest ways homeowners get stuck with incomplete work.

Permits are where homeowners should check early. For most residential properties, driveway paving typically does not require a building permit in many PA municipalities. However, the driveway approach—where the driveway meets the public road—may require a driveway access permit from your local municipality and/or PennDOT requirements. Before excavation, ask your contractor to confirm what applies in St. Thomas’s surrounding jurisdiction and to your specific road frontage.

Runoff matters too. Pennsylvania stormwater rules (including Chapter 102) generally require that paving projects do not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. That means proper grading, shoulder tie-ins, and drainage solutions should be part of the plan—not an afterthought.

Finally, protect yourself with liability verification. Confirm workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov, and request certificates of insurance before work starts. To verify step-by-step: (1) check PA HIC registration online, (2) ask for a current certificate of insurance (general liability) naming your property as certificate holder if available, (3) verify workers’ comp coverage via wcais.pa.gov, and (4) get the permit/approach requirements confirmed in writing when your driveway ties into a public roadway.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your St. Thomas driveway?

In St. Thomas, the freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor in the asphalt vs. concrete choice. Both materials move with temperature changes, but they fail differently under repeated winter cycles and the moisture that gets forced into the pavement structure. Asphalt is flexible, so it typically handles freeze-thaw stress well when the base is built correctly. The tradeoff is maintenance: asphalt usually needs sealcoating every 2–3 years to protect the binder and slow crack growth. Concrete is rigid and can last a long time, but it can spall or develop surface cracking when freeze-thaw cycling and road salt combine—especially if drainage isn’t controlled.

Local housing age pushes many homeowners toward full replacement sooner rather than later. With a large share of St. Thomas homes built before 1980 (63.1%, U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many existing driveways are at or beyond expected life. That’s why overlays may not be appropriate when the base has already moved—hiding the problem with a surface lift often shortens lifespan.

Where does the price difference make sense? If your driveway base is failing, a full asphalt replacement in the range of $4,500–$12,000 can be cheaper in the long run than repeated crack filling and patching. But if your current asphalt is sound and only the top surface is aged, resurfacing around $2,500–$7,000 can be a practical budget option.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in St. Thomas

What causes driveway cracks in St. Thomas?

In St. Thomas, driveway cracks are usually a combination of water intrusion and movement through the freeze-thaw cycle. When water seeps into tiny surface openings and the subbase, it expands in winter and contracts as temperatures rise, widening existing weaknesses. Older driveways—especially those installed when many homes were built before 1980—often have shallower or less consistent base, so the pavement has more room to shift. Heavy vehicle loads and poor drainage can accelerate cracking too, particularly where the driveway slope directs water toward joints and edges. If sealcoating was delayed, the asphalt binder can age and become more brittle, which makes cracks start sooner. That’s why crack filling alone is typically a short-term fix; it helps when cracks are minor and the structure underneath is stable.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in St. Thomas?

Sometimes, but it depends entirely on the cause of the damage and how the policy is written. In Pennsylvania, most policies won’t cover “wear and tear” or damage that develops gradually from freeze-thaw movement. However, if the driveway is damaged by a sudden covered event—like a vehicle impact, a covered storm, or a burst pipe from an emergency failure—there may be coverage. Your contractor can help by documenting conditions before and after work (photos, measured cracking areas, and a written scope explaining whether it’s structural failure, drainage issues, or surface distress). If you’re submitting a claim, ask the contractor whether they can provide an itemized repair plan that aligns with what the insurer requires. Even with a strong case, many claims end up focusing on the visible repair areas rather than underlying base problems—so it’s important to address the root cause in any paving proposal, not just the top cracks.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

There isn’t one universal number for every driveway in Pennsylvania because thickness depends on the subbase condition, expected traffic, and whether you’re overlaying or doing full replacement. For many St. Thomas residential driveways, asphalt thickness in a full-depth installation is commonly planned around the idea of placing enough material to span the freeze-thaw stress while resting on properly compacted stone. That’s why a full replacement often includes rebuilding the base (commonly 8–12 inches of compacted stone, depending on findings) and then paving in lifts. For overlay work, thickness is usually thinner because the existing asphalt and base are assumed to be structurally sound; that’s the condition that keeps overlay options in the $2,500–$7,000 range rather than jumping to full replacement. A reliable contractor will evaluate the base, explain whether an overlay is actually appropriate, and then specify lift thickness and compaction practices in the written scope.

How long does driveway paving take in St. Thomas?

The timeline in St. Thomas typically depends on how much work is required: overlay/resurfacing versus full tear-out and reconstruction. A simple asphalt resurfacing can often be completed within a short window of days once materials are scheduled, while full replacement includes excavation, hauling/disposal, base rebuild, paving in lifts, and cure time. Concrete tends to require longer scheduling because of curing and surface finishing steps. Beyond the job duration, plan for seasonal booking—reputable contractors in Franklin County are often scheduling projects about 3–8 weeks out in-season for replacements, and longer for larger driveway rebuilds. To get the timeline right, ask for a written arrival estimate, a completion window, and a cure-time note that explains when you can drive on the surface. Good planning matters in Pennsylvania freeze-thaw conditions because rushing compaction or allowing premature traffic can lead to early failures.

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in St. Thomas?

Signs of a failing driveway base often show up before the whole driveway collapses. Look for recurring cracking that comes back quickly after crack filling, depressions that form after rain, “alligator” cracking that spreads in a network, and edge breakup where the pavement crumbles away from the sides. Ponding or standing water is a major clue too, because the freeze-thaw cycle will repeatedly expand water under the pavement and accelerate movement. You may also notice pumping (a spongy feel) or areas that feel lower and bounce under tire loads. If you’ve had only surface treatments—like sealcoating or patching—that don’t stop the deterioration, the base may be the real issue. In Franklin County, base problems are commonly tied to drainage and older subbase conditions; with many St. Thomas homes built before 1980, underbuilt bases are not rare. When the base is failing, the most effective solution is usually full replacement in the $4,500–$12,000 band, not repeated resurfacing.

How much does driveway paving cost in St. Thomas, PA?

Typical 2026 budgeting for St. Thomas driveway paving is usually tied to whether you’re replacing, resurfacing, or doing preventive maintenance. For asphalt, full replacement with a new base commonly falls in the $4,500–$12,000 range, while asphalt overlay/resurfacing is often $2,500–$7,000 when the existing base is sound. Sealcoating on existing asphalt is commonly $350–$950 per application, depending on driveway size and surface condition. For concrete, new driveway installation is often $6,000–$16,000. If your driveway needs only minor stabilization, crack filling and patching can be around $200–$800. The reason your number can move within those bands is almost always scope: driveway size, how much excavation is required, drainage/grading corrections, and asphalt thickness. The best next step is an on-site evaluation so the quote matches whether you need overlay versus full reconstruction.
