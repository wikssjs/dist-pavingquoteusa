Driveway size is usually the biggest driver—material and labor rise directly with sq ft. But condition is where cost jumps. For example, a driveway with only hairline cracking may fit an overlay range like $1,800–$6,000, while the same-looking driveway with pumping, alligator cracking, or edge failure can push toward $4,500–$11,000 after tear-out and rebuild. Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, residential paving is usually straightforward, but the “who and how” matters as much as the asphalt. If the work exceeds $500, many driveway paving projects require the paving contractor to be registered as a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. You can verify status at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Also, after storms, avoid “storm chasers.” Any contractor soliciting driveway work door-to-door without a verifiable PA HIC registration is a significant red flag.

Permits: in most PA municipalities, driveway paving typically does not require a building permit, but the driveway approach—where your driveway meets the public road—may require driveway access approval from PennDOT or your local municipality. Check before excavation, especially if the apron or curb line is involved.

Water runoff matters. Pennsylvania stormwater requirements (commonly referenced under Chapter 102) require that paving projects do not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. That means grading must direct water properly and may require drainage solutions.

Liability protections are non-negotiable. Verify workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov (and confirm their certificate of insurance). Uninsured crews can expose you to risk.

Step-by-step for Hollidaysburg homeowners: (1) confirm HIC registration online, (2) request a certificate of insurance showing general liability, (3) request proof of workers’ compensation, and (4) check whether the street approach needs municipal or PennDOT access approval before anyone starts demo. If a contractor can’t provide documents quickly and clearly, pause and shop another bid.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Hollidaysburg driveway?

For Hollidaysburg homeowners, the asphalt-vs-concrete decision is largely driven by Pennsylvania’s freeze–thaw cycle and how your driveway sheds water. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail in different ways. Asphalt is flexible, so it generally handles freeze–thaw stress better and can tolerate minor movements without instantly fracturing across the entire slab. That said, asphalt depends on maintenance—especially sealcoating on a roughly 2–3 year rhythm—to protect against water intrusion and oxidation. Concrete is rigid and can perform very well, but in Blair County it can be more vulnerable to spalling or cracking when road salt and repeated freeze–thaw cycles attack the surface.

Because 78% of Hollidaysburg homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many driveways are at (or beyond) their original service life. That’s why full replacement is common here: when base and subbase are weakened, overlay-only fixes usually don’t last. Asphalt’s adaptability can be a strong value when the driveway has minor subgrade movement, while concrete can be worth it when you want long-term, low-routine maintenance and the subgrade can be rebuilt correctly for a rigid surface.

Here’s a concrete example: if your driveway is structurally sound and only needs resurfacing, asphalt overlay often lands in the $1,800–$6,000 range. If inspection shows base failure and you’re heading into full tear-out, asphalt replacement at $4,500–$11,000 may cost more upfront—but it’s the kind of rebuild that can eliminate the recurring cycle of patch-and-seal. Concrete replacement can run $6,000–$16,000, and it’s a better justification when you’re already doing full base work and want a longer service horizon.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in Hollidaysburg

Choosing a driveway contractor in Hollidaysburg is about verifying credentials and making sure the bid matches your actual site conditions. First, confirm licensing/registration. In Pennsylvania, contractors performing home improvement work should be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) when applicable—verify at the PA Attorney General’s HIC registry. Also ask whether they carry relevant certifications like IICRC-style credentials for repair/mitigation work (if they’re offering driveway restoration beyond standard paving). Then verify liability coverage and workers’ compensation before anyone starts: request certificates of insurance and proof of WC, and confirm WC status through Pennsylvania’s worker’s comp system at wcais.pa.gov.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. You want a breakdown that shows labor, materials, excavation/removal (if needed), base stone depth, asphalt thickness, and any drainage or grading. Avoid “lump sum only” bids that don’t explain what’s included. Read the scope for exclusions (for example: what happens if base excavation reveals more failure than expected, or whether hauling/disposal is included). If you’re using insurance for damage, ask whether they provide claim-support documentation.

Warranty matters: ask for workmanship warranty length and whether materials are separately warranted. For payment, never pay more than about 10–15% upfront; hold back the remainder until the job is complete and the final surface meets spec. Timeline should be written too: request an estimated start date, arrival window, and completion schedule, especially in peak season when Blair County contractors may have 4–8 week leads.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Hollidaysburg

Should I repair or replace my driveway in Hollidaysburg?

In Hollidaysburg, the right choice usually depends on what’s happening under the surface. If your driveway has minor cracking under about 1/4 inch and the base seems stable (no pumping, no sections that rock, and no major heaving), repairs or an asphalt overlay can extend service life. A crack filling and patching-only approach is generally a short-to-mid term fix, often lasting 1–3 years per application. If the pavement is failing due to base breakdown—common with older pre-1980 housing stock in the area (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—a full replacement is typically the durable solution. For homeowners seeing widespread alligator cracking, edge failure, or movement after snowmelt and salt seasons, replacement is commonly the only way to stop water-driven base deterioration. Cost-wise, an asphalt overlay/resurfacing often falls in the $1,800–$6,000 range, while full replacement with new base commonly lands in $4,500–$11,000. We recommend evaluating base condition first, because sealcoating alone won’t correct structural issues created by freeze–thaw cycling and weak subbases.

How do I find a reputable paving contractor in Hollidaysburg, PA?

Start by verifying credentials before you compare price. In Pennsylvania, many driveway projects where the total exceeds $500 require a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration; you can check at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Then request proof of general liability insurance and workers’ compensation—an uninsured crew can create liability for you if an accident happens. Next, ask for 2–3 itemized written quotes that spell out the actual scope: excavation/demo, stone depth, asphalt thickness/lifts, grading/drainage changes, and what happens if deeper base problems appear. Be cautious of bids that only quote one lump number or that blur the difference between overlay and full depth installation. A reliable contractor will also provide a workmanship warranty and be specific about timeline and start dates (lead times in Blair County can stretch 4–8 weeks in peak season). For a budgeting anchor, you can compare bids against typical bands such as $1,800–$6,000 for overlay and $4,500–$11,000 for full asphalt replacement, but make sure the cheaper quote isn’t skipping base preparation.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Hollidaysburg?

Sealcoating in Hollidaysburg is best thought of as preventive maintenance—useful when the asphalt is still structurally solid. A common recommendation is every 2–3 years for existing asphalt that’s aged, but only after the surface is cleaned and any active issues are repaired. If you’re seeing minor surface cracking, sealcoating can slow oxidation and reduce water intrusion. If your driveway has deep ruts, pumping, major alligator cracking, or noticeable heaving, sealcoating is not a cure; those are signs of base or structural failure and replacement is usually the correct long-term fix. Typical sealcoating budgets for a standard residential driveway often fall into the $300–$1,200 range per application, but the real determining factor is driveway size and how much prep is needed. Timing matters too: we avoid sealing during cold, wet conditions so the material can cure properly before spring freeze–thaw cycles return. If you tell your contractor how old the asphalt is and whether cracks are growing, they can recommend whether sealcoat should be your next step or whether the right move is overlay or replacement.

What causes driveway cracks in Hollidaysburg?

Driveway cracking in Hollidaysburg is usually a combination of climate and site conditions. The freeze–thaw cycle in Blair County exerts repeated expansion/contraction forces on the pavement. When water gets into the asphalt surface or the base, it freezes, expands, and can create heaving—leading to cracks that widen over time. Older housing stock is also a factor: with many homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), it’s common to find weakened stone subbases or drainage that no longer performs as originally designed. Clay soils and road salt exposure can worsen this by retaining moisture and degrading surface binders. Cracks can be “surface-only” (sometimes addressed with crack filling and patching) or structural (when cracks reflect base movement, which typically requires thicker asphalt, better base, and often full replacement). A local example: if you notice cracking paired with soft spots after snowmelt, that strongly suggests water-driven base issues rather than just aging asphalt. That’s why a proper inspection matters—sealcoating may improve appearance and protect the surface, but it won’t stop structural movement.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in Hollidaysburg?

Homeowner’s insurance coverage varies, and in Hollidaysburg it commonly depends on the cause of damage. Most standard policies are more likely to cover sudden, accidental events (for example: impact by a vehicle, a covered storm event where damage is clearly caused by a specific incident) rather than normal wear and tear from freeze–thaw aging. If your driveway is failing due to long-term base deterioration, cracking, or settlement that develops over years, insurers often classify that as deterioration rather than a covered loss. Freeze–thaw cycling and water infiltration are typical reasons driveways crack in Pennsylvania; those are generally not treated as a one-time “event.” If you believe your driveway damage is tied to a covered incident, ask your contractor to document the condition with photos and notes about observed distress (e.g., heaving, pumping, base failure indicators) so you can support the claim. Contractors who do this professionally can help you provide accurate information even if the outcome is ultimately determined by your carrier. As a practical budget anchor, homeowners often choose to repair or replace rather than wait, with crack filling/patching commonly in the $200–$800 range and full asphalt replacement often landing in $4,500–$11,000.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

In Pennsylvania, asphalt thickness is tied closely to climate exposure and, most importantly, base condition. In Hollidaysburg, the freeze–thaw cycle and road salt make it risky to go too thin, especially on older subbases. As a practical guideline in the Blair County market, many properly built residential driveways use about 3–4 inches of finished asphalt placed in two lifts (thickness can vary based on traffic load, slope, and the condition of the underlying base). For driveways where the base is truly sound, an overlay can be thinner than a full replacement, but overlay still depends on base stability—if the base is failing, overlay thickness won’t fix the root cause. When base preparation is required, we typically rebuild compacted stone to the needed depth (often 6–12" for many older Hollidaysburg properties, depending on conditions) so the asphalt can perform as designed. If you’re comparing bids, ask the contractor to specify the intended finished asphalt thickness and whether you’re doing an overlay/resurfacing or full depth installation. That’s how you ensure the quoted $1,800–$6,000 overlay option or the $4,500–$11,000 full replacement option matches your driveway’s actual needs.
