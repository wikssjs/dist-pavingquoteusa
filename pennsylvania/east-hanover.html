Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors who work above $500 generally must be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. If someone shows up after a storm and starts soliciting driveway work (“storm chasers”) without a verifiable PA HIC registration, treat it as a major red flag.

Permits: in most PA municipalities, residential driveway paving typically does not require a building permit for the asphalt itself. However, your driveway approach—the portion where the driveway meets the public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality, especially if any work affects curb lines, guiderails, or the road surface. Always check before excavation so you don’t get stuck mid-project.

Runoff and stormwater: Pennsylvania’s stormwater rules (Chapter 102) require that paving projects do not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. A reputable contractor will confirm grading and may include drainage solutions to keep water flowing where it belongs.

Also confirm workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can put liability on you.

Step-by-step for East Hanover: (1) look up the contractor’s HIC registration online, (2) request a current certificate of insurance (general liability and, if applicable, auto), (3) request proof of workers’ compensation, (4) confirm whether a driveway access/curb-cut permit is needed based on your exact location and approach, and (5) keep copies of everything with your contract and schedule.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your East Hanover driveway?

Pennsylvania’s freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor for many East Hanover homeowners. Asphalt and concrete both expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt is more flexible, so it tends to handle freeze-thaw movement with fewer catastrophic cracks; however, it needs maintenance. A proper sealcoating cycle every 2–3 years helps protect the binder, reduce water intrusion, and slow surface wear—especially with the salt and traction abrasives used during winter.

Concrete is rigid and durable when installed correctly, but it can spall or crack under heavy freeze-thaw cycling and road salt exposure if joints, subbase preparation, or thickness aren’t right. In Dauphin County, where winter freeze-thaw events are common, many older East Hanover driveways are already at or beyond their original lifespan. With about 42.3% of homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), a lot of pavements face a base/support reality problem, not just a “surface” problem—making full replacement more common than resurfacing when the base is failing.

A practical dollar example: if your driveway is structurally failing, choosing a concrete install at about $9,000–$18,000 can be worth it for homeowners who want a long-term, low-fuss surface. But if the base is solid and you have surface cracking only, an asphalt overlay in the $3,500–$9,000 band can be the better value. The wrong choice is treating sealcoating or crack filling as a fix for base failure—maintenance products can’t rebuild support or correct ponding.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in East Hanover

Choosing the right contractor in East Hanover starts with verifying credentials and then insisting on a clear, itemized proposal. Pennsylvania requires Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration for most jobs over $500—confirm it through the Attorney General’s HIC registry. For additional safety checks, verify workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov. If you see “paperwork optional” language or the contractor refuses to provide proof of insurance, walk away.

Next, get 2–3 written quotes that break out labor and materials rather than a single lump sum. Ask how much is for demo/removal, how deep they’re excavating, and whether they are replacing base with compacted stone. A responsible crew will also describe asphalt thickness (overlay versus full depth) and include what they will do for edging and drainage. Scope clarity matters: determine what’s excluded (for example, hauling/disposal, concrete apron replacements, or any landscaping restoration) so you don’t get surprise change orders later.

Warranty should be in writing: workmanship coverage length and what is included, plus materials warranty if applicable. Payment schedule matters too—never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and hold back funds until the job is complete and you’ve reviewed the final surface and grading. Finally, ask for a written arrival estimate and completion window so you can plan around East Hanover scheduling realities, including peak-season lead times.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in East Hanover

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in East Hanover?

In East Hanover, failing base usually shows up as more than just cracks. Look for settled or “soft” areas that feel lower than the surrounding pavement, edges that crumble or separate from the driveway border, and cracking that spreads in irregular patterns (often accompanied by patched areas that never seem to fully hold). Alligator cracking over a broad area can point to loss of base support, especially if cracks return quickly after patching. Another major indicator is recurring ponding: if water sits after rain, the freeze-thaw cycle can keep breaking down the foundation. If you see asphalt that looks thin, or you notice movement when snowplows pass, the underlying stone base may be inadequate. Finally, if your driveway needs repeated crack filling without lasting improvement, it’s often a sign that the base needs rebuilding, which is why full replacement projects in the $7,000–$15,000 range are frequently recommended when base failure is present.

How much does driveway paving cost in East Hanover, PA?

Driveway paving costs in East Hanover depend most on square footage, how much excavation is needed, and whether your project is an overlay or a full replacement. For asphalt full replacement (including new base), a common 2026 estimate band is $7,000–$15,000. If the existing base is sound and the issue is mostly surface cracking, resurfacing/overlay projects typically fall around $3,500–$9,000. Maintenance like sealcoating is separate and usually runs about $300–$900 per standard driveway application. Concrete driveway installs are commonly in the $9,000–$18,000 range. Home details matter too: East Hanover has a high owner-occupied rate (about 85.8% of households) and a large share of single-family homes (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), which means driveways are heavily used daily and often receive earlier replacement when they reach the limits of older base construction. If you want tighter pricing, the best approach is an on-site evaluation and an itemized quote that shows base depth and asphalt thickness.

How long does a new asphalt driveway last in East Hanover's climate?

A properly installed new asphalt driveway in East Hanover—built on adequate, compacted stone base and placed with correct thickness—typically lasts about 20–30 years under local freeze-thaw conditions. What makes the timeline real is the combination of base preparation and maintenance. If water can’t pond due to proper pitch and drainage, the asphalt experiences less movement during winter cycles. Sealcoating also plays a role: many homeowners choose sealcoating every 2–3 years to extend the service life by slowing oxidation and reducing water intrusion. That “maintenance cadence” is especially helpful in Dauphin County where winters can be hard on pavement and road salt is used. If you skip base repairs and only seal or fill cracks on a driveway with poor support, the asphalt may fail far sooner, often requiring overlay or replacement. When evaluating longevity, ask your contractor whether they’re planning a full depth install (commonly 3–4" asphalt in two lifts) versus an overlay, since that choice changes how long the pavement can resist structural movement.

Should I repair or replace my driveway in East Hanover?

In East Hanover, repair is usually the right move only when the problem is minor and surface-level—such as small cracks under about 1/4 inch with no evidence of base settlement or ongoing ponding. Crack filling and patching alone generally buys time, typically around 1–3 years per application, often with estimates in the $200–$800 range depending on the extent. Once you see broad alligator cracking, settled areas, or edge failure, the base support is commonly compromised and repairs can become a recurring cost. In those cases, homeowners usually face a choice between overlay and full replacement. Overlay/resurfacing is best when the base is truly solid and cracking is mostly on the surface, with a typical estimate around $3,500–$9,000. Full replacement is recommended when the base has failed, pushing driveway projects toward $7,000–$15,000. This matters because about 42.3% of East Hanover homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), and many of those older driveways were installed without modern base/drainage practices. Freeze-thaw cycles then amplify any foundational weakness.

How do I find a reputable paving contractor in East Hanover, PA?

Start by verifying contractor credentials before discussing pricing. You can verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Next, confirm workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Ask for a current certificate of insurance (general liability, and auto coverage if appropriate). When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in East Hanover?

For most East Hanover asphalt driveways, sealcoating is a preventive step applied every 2–3 years, assuming the pavement is still structurally sound. If your surface is aged but not settling, sealcoating can slow oxidation, reduce minor surface water infiltration, and help you stretch the life of the asphalt between larger projects. However, sealcoating is not a substitute for addressing base problems or active structural cracking. If you have widespread alligator cracking, areas that feel “spongy,” or ponding after rain, sealing over it usually fails to solve the root cause. That’s when you’re more likely looking at resurfacing/overlay (often around $3,500–$9,000 depending on scope) or full replacement (often $7,000–$15,000). If your asphalt is still new, timing depends on the manufacturer’s cure period and weather conditions, so ask your contractor for a seasonal schedule that matches East Hanover’s spring/fall pavement temperatures. A good plan is also to fill and address minor cracks before sealcoat so the sealer isn’t forced to bridge active movement.
