Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

For driveway paving in Upper Chichester, PA, start by confirming your contractor is properly registered and insured. In Pennsylvania, paving contractors who do Home Improvement work exceeding $500 must be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. You can verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be especially cautious if a contractor solicits driveway work door-to-door after storms (“storm chasers”)—without a verifiable PA HIC registration, it’s a major red flag.

Permits: residential driveway paving often does not trigger a building permit in many Pennsylvania municipalities, but the driveway approach—where your driveway meets the public road—commonly requires a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. That means grading/excavation near the road may need approval before work starts, even if the rest of your driveway does not.

Runoff matters. Pennsylvania stormwater requirements (commonly discussed under Chapter 102) expect paving not to increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. A competent crew will re-establish proper slope and may include drainage solutions so meltwater and rain don’t pool.

Also verify workers’ compensation at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can expose you to liability if an injury happens on your property.

Step-by-step for Upper Chichester homeowners: (1) check HIC registration online, (2) request a certificate of insurance naming you as additional insured (general liability), (3) confirm workers’ comp coverage, and (4) ask whether a PennDOT or municipal driveway access permit is required before excavation and curb work.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Upper Chichester driveway?

In Upper Chichester, PA, the freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor for many homeowners choosing asphalt versus concrete. Asphalt is flexible, which helps it tolerate movement from temperature swings; when maintained with sealcoating, it tends to resist freeze-thaw cracking better. Concrete is rigid and can last a long time, but it can spall or crack under repeated freeze-thaw stress and road salt exposure—especially when drainage is imperfect or water sits at low spots. With Delaware County winters bringing multiple freeze-thaw cycles per season, proper base prep and drainage often matter more than the choice of surface alone.

Upper Chichester’s housing stock also pushes many homes toward replacement rather than thin fixes. With about 60.3% of homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), it’s common to find failing stone base, patchwork repairs, or settled edges that make “overlay only” risky. In those cases, full asphalt replacement is frequently the most cost-effective long-term approach, while concrete becomes more attractive if you want lower routine maintenance and can address drainage upfront.

To put the dollars in context: an asphalt overlay/resurfacing project often runs about $2,800–$7,000 when the base is sound, while a full concrete driveway installation is commonly around $7,000–$18,000. The price difference is justified when your current asphalt is structurally compromised (replacement makes more sense) or when you prefer concrete’s long service life and are willing to pay for the right base and jointing strategy from day one.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in Upper Chichester

Choosing the right contractor matters as much as the material. Start by verifying IICRC certification and Pennsylvania licensing using online registries. Pennsylvania requires HIC registration for home improvement work above $500—check hic.attorneygeneral.gov for the contractor’s HIC status and business details. For workers and crew competency (and for specialized services like removal/repair), ask what certifications apply and confirm them through the issuing registry.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. For paving, “lump sum” isn’t enough. Your quote should list labor and materials (tearout/disposal, base stone, asphalt/concrete thickness, compaction, edging/apron work, and any drainage regrading). Make sure the scope clearly states what’s excluded—common exclusions include unstated base failures, drainage correction, or curb apron restoration. Ask whether photos of damaged areas are documented and whether insurance claim documentation is supported if you’re working with a carrier. Also request the warranty in writing: workmanship warranty length, plus any equipment/material warranty the supplier provides.

Payment schedule is another key. Never pay more than 10–15% upfront; hold back the remainder until the job is fully complete and cleaned. Timeline matters too: ask for an arrival estimate and a completion window in writing, since peak spring and fall scheduling in Delaware County can extend lead times for reputable crews.

What causes driveway cracks in Upper Chichester?

Driveway cracking in Upper Chichester is usually a combination of water, movement, and base performance over time. Pennsylvania’s freeze-thaw cycle is the main repeat offender: water enters small cracks, freezes, expands, and gradually breaks up the pavement. If the driveway has ponding due to low spots or poor slope, the problem accelerates because more water sits on the surface. Another common cause is an inadequate or failing base—especially in older homes, and about 60.3% of local dwellings were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024). When subgrade isn’t properly compacted or drainage is poor, settlement and voids form, which reflect through the asphalt as cracking and patchwork failure. Heavy vehicle loads and sharp turning can also contribute, particularly near the garage apron. Finally, skipping maintenance like sealcoating can allow oxidation, making cracks easier to form. Crack filling around $200–$800 can help early, but if cracking is widespread, you’ll likely need overlay or full replacement.
