Johnstown paving prices by scope — 2026

In Johnstown, where more than half of homes are single-family and the homeownership rate sits around 52.0% (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), driveways are a significant expense for most property owners—not a shared building cost. Paving in Cambria County is shaped by our long, snowy winters and frequent freeze-thaw cycles. Shallow shale, rocky subgrade, and high ground snow loads make proper base construction far more critical here than in milder climates.

The single biggest driver of price between contractors is what they do—often don’t do—under the surface. While it’s tempting to focus on inches of asphalt or concrete thickness, the real money is spent (or wasted) on excavation, drainage, and the depth and quality of the aggregate base. A cut-rate job might look fine on day one but will show its faults in a Johnstown winter within just a few seasons. Always judge bids by the scope of work beneath the surface, not just the surface finish or price-per-square-foot.

Choosing a paving company

In Cambria County, and especially Johnstown, picking the right paving contractor means more than finding the lowest bid. The region’s network of established asphalt plants and quarries means your contractor should know the local suppliers; if they can’t name their source in Johnstown or Ebensburg, you may be dealing with an out-of-town broker. Local companies with a business address—not just a cell number—are far less likely to disappear after the first frost heave shows their shortcuts. Also, equipment ownership is key. Long-term contractors own their own pavers, rollers, and trucks—it’s rarely a good sign if everything arrives with a rental tag.

References matter, but insist on examples three years or older. Most problems with driveways in our area take two or three cycles of freeze-thaw to show.

  • Verify Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration via hic.attorneygeneral.gov.
  • Demand proof of workers’ compensation through WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov).
  • Ask for a business address in Cambria County, not just a phone number.
  • Check for company-owned equipment, not all rentals.
  • Ask which asphalt plant or quarry supplies their material.
  • Request references from jobs three or more years old in Johnstown or nearby towns.
  • Insist on written estimates showing base depth and materials used.
  • Look for clear, detailed contracts—required by the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act over $500.
  • Be wary of “today only” discounts or pressure to pay in cash up front.
  • Listen for vague answers about permits or drainage design: that’s how shortcuts start.
  • A proper contractor will never offer to waive your insurance deductible (it’s illegal in Pennsylvania).

Red flags: crews going door-to-door, refusal to provide registration numbers, no local reference jobs older than last season, or avoid answering who supplies their asphalt. Genuine Johnstown contractors will answer those questions plainly and provide documentation up front.

Drainage: the failure nobody plans for

Most driveway failures in Johnstown aren’t caused by surface wear—they’re caused by water and the region’s relentless freeze-thaw cycles. Here, grading is not a cosmetic touch: it’s what keeps your driveway from turning into a roller coaster in two winters. The driveway should slope at least 2% (a quarter inch per foot) away from buildings for proper runoff. In narrow mill-era lots, cross-slope or slight crowning is used to guide water toward gutters or yard drains, not the garage or foundation, where infiltration causes frost heave and base failure. If regrading isn’t possible, especially in older Johnstown homes, trench drains or channel drains at the low end may be necessary—with the outflow “daylighting” on your property or diverting to a dry well, but never onto your neighbor’s land (it’s a liability no matter what the permit reviewer missed).

Heavy shale and shallow rocky subgrade in Cambria Township increase ponding risk and make drainage even more critical—poorly-drained heavy soils keep water near the base, where it freezes and heaves the slab from below. This is compounded by Cambria County’s snow loads and freeze depth of 36 inches, so any misrouted runoff is soon a much bigger problem.

Always ask your contractor to explain how drainage will be managed. If the only plan is “slope it a bit,” that’s not going to hold up here. The extra work for drainage—grading, edge drains, or trenching—may cost more on the bid, but it will save the driveway itself.

Sealcoating: what it does and what it doesn't

Sealcoating is one of those services in Johnstown that gets pushed hard, most often by companies that don’t do excavation or base work. Here’s what it really does: A quality sealcoat helps prevent oxidation of the asphalt binder, protects against dripped oil and fuel, and gives that deep black “like new” look. But sealcoating does not add structural strength, fix cracking, or extend the life of a driveway whose base is already compromised by freeze-thaw cycles or water infiltration.

The part most contractors don’t say: sealing too often gives you a surface of brittle layers that crack and peel off. In this climate, every couple of years is reasonable, and the first seal should wait until your new asphalt has cured—usually a full season—so trapped solvents can escape. Spraying a thin, watery coat or sealing every year is not just a waste, it can shorten the life of your driveway by trapping moisture underneath.

For Cambria County, the maintenance that matters most is crack filling—done right with a hot, flexible compound, it keeps water out of the base. Sealcoating is a value-add, but not a substitute. Pricing typically runs $175–$650 per visit, but watch out for quotes far below that: it usually means a cut-rate spray job with little prep or protection. Proper sealcoating means edges are brushed in and cracks filled; any less and you’re paying mostly for optics.

What's under the asphalt matters more than the asphalt

In Johnstown, what you see on the surface barely hints at what makes a driveway last—or fail. Every decent asphalt driveway starts from the bottom up: a shaped subgrade, a compacted crushed stone aggregate base, and then hot asphalt in multiple lifts on top. Each layer has a job. The base distributes the weight of cars and snow plows, while the asphalt only covers and seals it. Put another way, a thick, beautiful asphalt layer over a thin or loose base will crumble and crack after our region’s first serious freeze-thaw cycle.

Local subgrade matters, especially in Cambria Township where shallow shale and rocky soil demand thicker crushed aggregate—at least 8 inches—compared to the 4–6 inches used in milder states. If your contractor isn't spelling out the type and depth (for example, 2A modified stone, compacted in 3–4 inch lifts), assume they plan to skimp. Johnstown’s repeated freezing, with frost reaching 36 inches deep, means any water held in clay or between rocks expands, lifts the base, and cracks the driveway from below.

The shortcuts always happen here. “Just resurfacing,” little or no excavation, thin aggregate, skipping compaction or testing—none of this is visible after the new surface is rolled and the trucks are gone. The heartbreak comes year three: cracks, sunken spots, and edge collapse. Demand specifics in writing: minimum aggregate base depth (typically at least 8 inches in rocky spots), aggregate type (2A or PennDOT-approved), and compaction done in 3–4 inch lifts with vibratory rollers. If your contractor won’t commit on paper, that’s your cue to move on.

How to tell if the base is gone

Before you decide on any paving scope in Johnstown, get a sense of your base condition. You don’t need x-ray vision—just an honest look. If you see alligator cracking (patches of small, broken lines like reptile skin) across large areas, the base has failed. Overlaying with new asphalt over that will only buy you a year at most. Surface-only cracks, especially long, straight ones, indicate aging or shrinkage that can be sealed and maintained. Birdbaths and depressions that collect water are the calling card of subgrade settlement or frost heave. Crumbling edges mean the original install was light on base or lacked edge support altogether. Potholes mean water has reached the foundation—don’t waste money patching these unless you address how the water got there in the first place.

Never accept an overlay bid from a contractor who hasn’t probed the base or explained these details. Money spent on a surface fix over a dead base is money gone. Assess the signs:

Driveway permits, approaches and stormwater rules

In Johnstown city limits, if you’re simply resurfacing your existing driveway—no expansion, no change to curb cuts, and no re-routing of stormwater—there is generally no building permit required. That saves you the bureaucracy for straightforward replacement work. However, if you’re putting in a new driveway, expanding, or changing the entrance at the curb or street, you’ll need zoning or building permit review through the City of Johnstown Codes Department. For state roads in Cambria County, any new or modified driveway approach requires a Highway Occupancy permit from PennDOT District 10. For township roads, it’s your local municipal office, such as Adams Township or Cambria Township.

Johnstown’s heavy rain and snow mean any increase in driveway size (impervious surface) can bring stormwater management into play. If water from your new drive ends up flooding your neighbor, you are liable—permits don’t override that. Always make sure your contractor explains how runoff stays on your property and meets local drainage requirements.

All driveway paving contracts over $500 require a written agreement under Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, and contractors must be registered with the Office of Attorney General (verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov). Workers’ comp is mandatory for all employers—check this at WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov). The right contractors will have these documents ready without hesitation.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Johnstown

Why does my driveway have depressions that hold water? In Johnstown, driveway depressions or “birdbaths” are usually the product of subgrade movement or freeze-thaw cycles heaving sections of the base. Our region’s frost depth of 36 inches and regular winter-thaw cycles make this almost inevitable without a well-compacted, thick aggregate base. Water infiltrates through surface cracks, saturates the subgrade—especially in clay-heavy or rocky soil like we see in Cambria Township—and on freezing, expands and shifts the base out of level. Over time, these low spots collect rainwater, meltwater, and debris. Unfortunately, patching depressions without fixing the underlying base is a temporary solution at best. A proper fix may require excavation and base replacement, which is reflected in the price bracket of $3,100–$4,900 for most Johnstown driveways requiring serious subgrade rehabilitation.
When can I seal a brand new asphalt driveway? Sealcoating a fresh asphalt drive in Johnstown should never be done right away. The bitumen binder in new asphalt needs to cure and release volatile oils—if you seal too early, you trap those oils, causing the surface to soften or turn gray and clouded. Given Cambria County’s climate, where cooler spring and fall temperatures extend curing times, you’re best off waiting until the next paving season—a good rule is at least 3 to 12 months after installation, with a full summer of sun. Rushed sealcoating won’t deliver the deep black, durable finish you want and might actually damage new pavement. When you do finally apply, expect a cost from $175–$650, but most critical is the timing, not just the price.
When is the best time of year to pave in Johnstown? The ideal paving window in Johnstown runs from late April to early October, when temperatures are warm enough to allow for proper compaction and curing. Avoid mid-winter and late fall bids, when cold nights cause asphalt to cool before it can be worked and lead to premature failures—especially with our local frost depths of up to 36 inches. Spring work can get delayed by saturated soils and freeze-thaw cycles, so mid-to-late summer installations often yield the most durable results for both asphalt and concrete driveways. Pricing, especially for a full asphalt replacement ($3,100–$8,100), reflects both the limited season and local demand, so expect cost variability at each end of the paving calendar.
Are pavers worth the cost in north-central Pennsylvania? While paver driveways offer undeniable curb appeal, they are rarely the best fit for Johnstown’s demanding climate. Many local homes built before 1980 lack the wide approaches or regular shapes pavers love, and our freeze-thaw cycles with deep frost can shift and settle paver beds unless excavation and drainage are perfect. As a result, long-term maintenance often outweighs the visual benefit—resetting and relaying lost stones due to frost heave is time-consuming. In this area, the upfront investment will generally be much higher than even the top of the concrete range ($4,500–$11,000), with little added resilience. Some homeowners may still want the look, but for value per dollar and practical durability, asphalt or poured concrete remain dominant in Cambria County.
What does sealcoating cost in Johnstown? In Johnstown, a single application of quality sealcoat on a residential driveway generally costs $175–$650 as of the 2026 season. Where you land in that range depends mostly on driveway size (single-lane vs. wide pad), the amount of prep needed, and whether crack filling is included. Quality differences matter more than initial price in this climate—cold-applied, watery coatings that don’t address existing cracks won’t last more than a season or two here, especially with our repeated freeze-thaw cycles. The most cost-effective approach is every two to four years, not annually. Too-frequent sealcoating leads to unsightly peeling in the Johnstown climate, so balance maintenance and cost for the best results.
What slope does a driveway need for drainage? For proper drainage in Johnstown, a driveway should slope away from the garage or house at a minimum of 2%—that’s roughly a quarter inch per foot. Our region’s freeze-thaw conditions and occasional heavy storms mean that anything flatter than this can create standing water, leading to freeze expansion, subgrade damage, and eventual surface failure. In older neighborhoods with narrow, shared drives from the early 1900s, effective drainage design might also require cross-slope or local trench drains if there’s no natural path. Adding the proper slope costs little upfront but can spare you a full replacement in as little as three winters. In terms of cost, insufficient drainage can turn a regular overlay job ($1,350–$4,000) into a full replacement down the road.
Do I need a permit to repave a driveway in Johnstown? For most homeowners in the City of Johnstown, simply resurfacing an existing asphalt driveway—no expansion, no change to curb, no grade alteration—does not require a building permit or zoning review. This saves both time and expense compared to new or expanded driveways, which almost always trigger municipal oversight. That said, if you intend to widen your existing drive, modify its entrance, or alter stormwater runoff, expect a permit request from the City of Johnstown Codes Department. For driveways that access state roads, a separate approach permit from PennDOT District 10 is required. Contractors must be registered through the Home Improvement Contractor registry (hic.attorneygeneral.gov) for contracts over $500. Never accept an upgrade or address change without paperwork.
What does an asphalt overlay cost in Johnstown? An asphalt overlay—where fresh asphalt is laid over an intact, stable base—typically runs from $1,350–$4,000 for a residential Johnstown driveway in 2026. The wide spread reflects variables like square footage, prep work (cleaning, minor patches, or crack filling), and edge treatments. Overlays make sense only if the underlying base is solid, showing no widespread alligator cracking or subgrade movement. If contractors offer overlay quotes without checking the base or probing for soft spots, be wary—a cheap overlay on a broken base will crack within a Johnstown winter or two. Done right, overlays can add 8–15 years of life; done wrong, it’s money that leaks away with the next thaw.
Are door-to-door pavers a problem in Johnstown? Door-to-door pavers appear in Johnstown every paving season, especially after big storms or near the end of season when crews claim to have “leftover asphalt.” Local homeowners should remain cautious—many of these crews work without proper Home Improvement Contractor registration, can’t provide proof of workers’ compensation, and disappear within weeks, leaving subpar work behind. They may offer prices too good to be true, sometimes even quoting far under the lowest band ($1,350 for an overlay) to get quick cash. In Cambria County, reputable contractors have verifiable registration (hic.attorneygeneral.gov), local references, and stable addresses. If you’re feeling pressured or the paperwork is vague, take it as your cue to pass—there’s no rush worth risking a failed driveway a year later.
