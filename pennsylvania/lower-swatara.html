Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, your contractor matters as much as the pavement. If the total job exceeds $500, paving contractors are typically required to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before work starts, verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be especially cautious of “storm chaser” contractors—door-to-door solicitation after storms—because lack of a verifiable PA HIC registration is a significant red flag.

Permits depend on what’s being touched. Residential driveway paving often does not require a building permit in many PA municipalities, but the driveway approach where your driveway meets the public road can require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. If the plan includes regrading, modifying the approach, work near curb lines, or changes affecting stormwater, check before excavation.

Water runoff is regulated under Pennsylvania stormwater requirements (commonly referenced as Chapter 102). Your project can’t increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way; reputable crews will grade correctly and may include drainage solutions.

Also verify liability and workers’ compensation coverage. Confirm workers’ compensation through wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can leave homeowners exposed to injury liability and job-site claims.

Step-by-step for Lower Swatara homeowners: (1) confirm HIC registration online; (2) request a current certificate of insurance for general liability; (3) request proof of workers’ compensation and verify it at wcais.pa.gov; (4) ask whether a driveway access permit is required for the approach and whether any stormwater review applies; and (5) get the permit determination (or “not required” statement) in writing before starting.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Lower Swatara driveway?

In Lower Swatara, the Pennsylvania freeze-thaw cycle is the defining factor in asphalt versus concrete decisions. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they typically fail in different ways. Asphalt is more flexible, so it often tolerates freeze-thaw movement better and can shed surface cracking if the base and drainage are correct. Concrete is rigid and can be a long-term solution, but it’s more sensitive to salt exposure and can spall or crack when freeze-thaw happens repeatedly in the presence of moisture.

Because 47% of Lower Swatara homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many driveways are reaching or exceeding their expected service life. In those cases, homeowners often get the best durability from full replacement asphalt builds where the base is rebuilt (commonly targeting 8–12 inches of compacted stone base and 3–4 inches of asphalt in two lifts). Resurfacing can be appropriate when the base is sound and cracking is mostly on the surface; if the base is compromised, an overlay can fail sooner and cost more long-term.

Here’s where the money usually makes sense. If your driveway is mostly stable with a sound base and only surface wear, resurfacing in the $3,500–$9,000 range can be a practical upgrade. But if you’re dealing with settled sections, widespread alligator cracking, or evidence of water-related base failure, full replacement is typically the better path—even though it’s commonly $7,000–$15,000. For concrete, new installations frequently fall in the $9,000–$18,000 band and are most attractive when you want a low-maintenance driveway and can accept that concrete repairs may be more visible after freeze-thaw seasons.

Should I repair or replace my driveway in Lower Swatara?

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Lower Swatara?

For many Lower Swatara asphalt driveways, sealcoating should be done as preventive maintenance every 2–3 years, as long as the pavement is structurally sound. The best timing is usually when surface wear is noticeable—fading color, minor surface oxidation, and small areas of dryness—yet before cracks become widespread and connected through the surface. Sealcoating won’t fix failed base or major settlement; it’s meant to protect asphalt from moisture and oxidation.

In practical terms, if your driveway has mostly surface cracking and no signs of pumping or significant settlement, sealcoating is commonly budgeted around $300–$900 for a standard residential driveway application. If you’re currently planning a larger upgrade, it’s also worth coordinating timing: homeowners sometimes schedule sealcoating right after resurfacing or after minor patching so the new surface gets protected sooner.

Ask your paving contractor to evaluate drainage and base condition first—then sealcoating is part of a longer-term plan, rather than a substitute for necessary base repair.

What causes driveway cracks in Lower Swatara?

Driveway cracks in Lower Swatara are typically caused by a combination of moisture, base movement, and temperature cycling. Pennsylvania’s freeze-thaw cycle expands water in the pavement layers and can lead to heaving when water isn’t allowed to drain away. When older driveways have thin asphalt over shallow stone—or when the driveway pitch directs water toward the house or toward low spots—cracks can spread faster and deeper.

Another common cause is inadequate base preparation. If the stone base wasn’t compacted properly or drainage was overlooked, the subgrade can shift over seasons. You’ll often see “early return” cracking after patch jobs when the problem is structural, not surface-level. Salt exposure contributes too: deicers can increase brittleness at the surface over time, making existing micro-cracks widen.

Finally, loading patterns matter. Driveways that receive frequent heavy vehicle traffic can stress weak spots, especially at the approach where turning and braking concentrate weight. The key is figuring out whether cracking is superficial—where crack filling and patching can help—or whether it reflects base and drainage failures that justify overlay or full replacement.

However, Lower Swatara homeowners may see coverage for sudden, accidental damage depending on the cause—such as a tree falling, a vehicle impact, or certain storm-related events—subject to your specific policy terms and deductibles. Insurance claims for driveway damage can also be limited if the damage is considered maintenance-related.

If you’re dealing with damage after a specific incident, document it thoroughly: photos before and after if possible, dates of the event, and any evidence of how the damage happened (impact, standing water from a known failure, erosion). Then ask your contractor for appropriate documentation—some paving projects include descriptions of observed conditions and the scope of repair/replacement needed.
