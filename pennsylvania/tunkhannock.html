What a real paving quote contains

Comparing paving quotes in Tunkhannock (or anywhere in Monroe County) should never be done off a single line and a total at the bottom. Any proper bid states the measured square footage. It tells you how deep the contractor plans to excavate, what happens to the old pavement, and details the new aggregate base: type of stone and depth in inches. A complete quote specifies how the base is compacted (not just “rolled,” but how many lifts and by what method). For the asphalt, you should see the number of layers (courses), the compacted depth of each, and the exact mix used. Drainage and site grading get their own lines—a driveway that pitches water toward your house, or a neighbor’s, will cost you more in the long run.

Cleanup matters: ask how they’ll restore the edges and lawn disturbed during work. The payment schedule should involve a small deposit (usually no more than one-third), with the rest due only after the job is fully completed. A warranty should be in writing, but know that most exclude cracking—what many expect to be covered—since that is considered maintenance and not a workmanship defect.

Beware the all-too-common quote that says nothing but "asphalt paving — $X." If you can't compare line-by-line, you can't compare at all. That is the main way folks in Tunkhannock end up paying full price for the cheapest job possible.

Driveway permits, approaches and stormwater rules

If you’re repaving an existing Tunkhannock driveway without changing its footprint, you usually won’t need a building permit—especially if you’re in one of the more rural Monroe County townships, where minor patching and maintenance work like sealcoating or crack filling isn’t reviewed by code enforcement. However, enlarging a driveway or installing new pavement altogether is a different matter. In places like Stroudsburg Borough, any new or expanded driveway generally means you’ll face a zoning or building permit review. It’s always wise to check with municipal code enforcement before you start tearing up asphalt or adding concrete.

The most overlooked permitting issue is the driveway approach—where your drive meets a public road. This almost always requires a separate permit and, on major routes like PA Route 611, is governed not by the township but by PennDOT District 5. Overstepping here can mean fines or a teardown at your own expense.

Any increase in impervious surface (like making the driveway wider) can trigger stormwater requirements, and no matter what your permit says, draining water across a neighbor’s property makes you liable for runoff issues or basement flooding. Detailed grading and water management should be in your written contract.

Finally, Pennsylvania requires that all paving contractors performing jobs over $500 hold a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration, which you can verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Any legitimate employer must show you valid workers' compensation coverage, verifiable via WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov). Don’t hire anyone unwilling to confirm these.

Overlay, patch or full replacement?

The right fix for your Tunkhannock driveway depends on what’s happening under the surface — not just the cracks you see. Here’s how you can diagnose it yourself, before picking up the phone. If your driveway shows widespread “alligator” cracking, where the pavement is broken up like the scales on a reptile’s back, you’re looking at a failed base. No amount of top-layer patching or overlay will last on top of that; you need a full replacement. When you see clean, linear cracks that are narrow (under a quarter inch), that’s usually ordinary surface aging, and crack filling and sealcoating are both suitable and cost-effective.

Depressions (birdbaths) collecting water hint at movement or sinking in the subgrade. This usually means localized excavation and a rebuild, otherwise the next winter will bring those dips back. Crumbling along the edges points to a missing or inadequate stone shoulder—edge repair or the addition of curbing will hold things together. Potholes, finally, always mean water has gotten down to the base layers. Cut out and patch down to solid stone before sealcoating, or you’ll simply trap more water.

Overlays have their place, but only on a solid, stable base. If a contractor quotes one without probing the base with a core or test hole, they haven’t actually checked—and you’ll pay for it in a few years when the surface fails again.

Why you can't pave in February

In Tunkhannock and across Monroe County, paving is forced to follow the seasons by more than just tradition. Our winter design temperature bottoms out at 4 °F, and local asphalt plants shut down completely for part of the year. Hot-mix asphalt needs to be applied at temperatures that allow for proper compaction — below about 50 °F, the mix cools too quickly and doesn’t compact tightly enough for long-term durability. You simply can’t get the right finish when the ground or air is cold, and the nearest suppliers around Stroudsburg and Tobyhanna won’t even load a truck in winter.

Concrete faces its own constraint: it can’t cure properly if it freezes before it sets up, so above-freezing overnight temps are non-negotiable.

Practically, the paving season runs from mid-April into October. Spring and fall are best if you want to catch contractors with schedule openings and, sometimes, better pricing. Summer is peak season — crews book up and you’ll pay a premium. If a company tries to sell you on a February or late November install, they are ignoring climate realities or running out the last of a bad season’s mix. Either way, that tells you plenty about the quality of the job you’ll get, and the odds you’ll see warranty support down the line.

Base preparation: the part you pay for and never see

The base under your Tunkhannock driveway is what makes or breaks it over the long term, especially given our rocky, glacial soils and winters that drive frost three feet into the ground. The assembly is simple to describe but easy to skimp: you start with subgrade (the native soil, dug down to stable material), then install a compacted aggregate base (usually crushed stone), then finish with one or more layers of asphalt. Every layer helps distribute the weight of vehicles, but it’s the base that prevents the whole thing from shifting and cracking as the ground moves or water freezes below.

Frost heave is a real risk here—when water in the subgrade freezes, it expands and raises the pavement in unpredictable ways, often cracking or lifting it above. Once the thaw comes, the pavement settles unevenly, turning small issues into major repairs. That’s why the depth of the aggregate base and its compaction are the most important numbers in your contract. For the rocky soils on the Pocono Plateau and around Tunkhannock, contractors should be specifying at least 6–8 inches of clean, compacted stone for driveways—more if the existing soil is poor or the driveway is extra long.

A thinner base, loose fill, or “paving over” old, broken asphalt is the oldest way to cheapen a quote. All of these problems lie hidden until you hit winter number two or three. The right contractor should put in writing: the base depth (in inches), the exact aggregate to be used (not just “stone” or “gravel”), and proof that compaction was done in lifts—a few inches at a time, tested before the next goes down. If you don’t see those details, you’re gambling with a lot more than just the driveway’s appearance.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Tunkhannock

What kind of soil is under Tunkhannock driveways?

Most driveways in Tunkhannock and northern Monroe County sit over glacial till—a rocky, compacted mix left behind as the glaciers moved through this part of Pennsylvania. There’s a lot of stone and clay, which can provide decent bearing if compacted but is notorious for holding water in lower spots. That’s why local driveways often require an extra-thick aggregate base for stability. If your old driveway is settling or catching water, it likely didn’t get enough base or drainage on day one. A full replacement job here means excavating down to solid material before building up with at least 6–8 inches of compacted crushed stone. This attention to local soil is the difference between a driveway that lasts 25 years, and one that’s riddled with birdbaths after two winters. Expect pricing at the higher end of the local bands: $3,400–$8,800 for a proper, durable full-depth replacement.

How much does it cost to widen a driveway?

Widening a driveway in Tunkhannock generally means new excavation, a full-depth base in the new section, and a tie-in to the old pavement. Because you’re not just resurfacing but adding new impervious surface, you might need a township permit for the expansion and a review of stormwater drainage. Expect to pay rates similar to full replacement for any new area—so the added width will price out at about $3,400–$8,800 for a full-depth asphalt section, pro-rated to the additional square footage. If you’re just adding a couple feet on one side, sometimes a reinforced edge and patching approach can work, but proper sub-base is always key. Cheap shortcuts—like layering hot mix straight onto sod—will fail fast in our freeze-thaw cycle. Get it specified in your contract: depth, aggregate, compaction, and the transition detail to old pavement.

What causes concrete to spall or flake in Tunkhannock?

Concrete driveways in Tunkhannock see a lot of freeze-thaw stress, plus the relentless use of deicing salts in winter. Spalling—where the surface flakes away—is most often caused by water getting into the surface pores, then freezing and popping off thin chips of concrete with each cycle. Poor finishing or improper curing during installation will make it worse. Salts accelerate this by breaking down the surface even faster. In northeastern Pennsylvania’s climate, where the heating season is long and temperatures swing wildly in spring and fall, concrete is under real stress. Quality installs are much more likely to survive if the slab is air-entrained and sealed. Replacement is the only real fix for significant spalling, and that will run in the $4,900–$11,500 range for a standard-size drive.

Are pavers worth the cost in northeastern Pennsylvania?

Interlocking pavers look great, but in Tunkhannock’s climate—where deep frost heave and drifting Poconos snow are the norm—they’re only worth the investment if you commit to thorough base preparation and upkeep. Without a deep, well-compacted aggregate base, frost will push and shift the pavers, leading to uneven surfaces or tripping hazards. Repairs are relatively easy (since you can pull and reset sections), but the initial install costs typically exceed concrete or high-quality asphalt. Pavers will often run higher than concrete's $4,900–$11,500 band once all labor and material is included. They can deliver top-notch curb appeal and good longevity—if you budget for periodic releveling and edge maintenance. For most Tunkhannock homeowners looking for low maintenance and long service in freeze-thaw, asphalt or concrete is a better value.

How far ahead should I book a paver in Tunkhannock?

Local paving companies in Tunkhannock and Monroe County tend to get booked up quickly, especially for new driveways or full-depth replacements. The season here is short—most work gets done between April and October, when hot-mix asphalt is available. For new installs or big resurfacing jobs, plan to book at least 1–3 months out during peak summer season. Calling in March or early April can get you on the calendar before prices climb and lead times stretch. Smaller jobs like patching or sealcoating (which run $200–$700) sometimes can fit into gaps between bigger projects. If a contractor can “fit you in next week” for a major job in mid-July, ask why—they may be over-promising or struggling for work. Quality companies will lay out a clear, realistic timeline from bid to completion.

Do I need a permit to repave a driveway in Tunkhannock?

For most Tunkhannock homeowners, repaving an existing driveway in its current footprint typically does not require a new building permit—especially if you live in a rural part of Monroe County. However, if you're planning to enlarge or move the driveway, or if you're on a major road like PA Route 611, additional permits (often from PennDOT District 5) are likely required. Always check with your local code enforcement office to confirm—requirements change between boroughs and townships. Any substantial expansion will trigger a closer look for stormwater management and runoff rules. Permits cost time and money but save headaches; an unpermitted approach can force costly rework. Budget-wise, repaving does not add to the cost—standard rates apply: $1,450–$4,400 for an overlay, $3,400–$8,800 for a full replacement—but unpermitted work can risk fines or forced removal.

How much does driveway paving cost in Tunkhannock?

The cost of paving a driveway in Tunkhannock depends on size, current condition, and what lies under the surface. For a typical residential single-family asphalt driveway—removal of old pavement, new base, and fresh asphalt—you’re looking at $3,400–$8,800, with most jobs coming out near the $5,400 mark. Overlaying an existing, stable base runs less, $1,450–$4,400. Full-depth concrete is higher, at $4,900–$11,500. Maintenance work such as sealcoating is the most affordable, at $200–$700 per application. Long rural driveways or those over poor subgrade (common on the Pocono Plateau) will be priced at the higher end because of extra base prep and haul time. Get written quotes specifying square footage, base specs, and include all site work in the total.

What slope does a driveway need for drainage?

Good drainage is non-negotiable for a durable driveway in Tunkhannock’s wet, freeze-prone climate. The standard target is at least a 2% slope, or about a quarter inch drop per foot, moving water away from garages and foundations and off the surface to suitable runoff areas. On longer drives, you’ll often see a slight crown in the center sloped to each side. If your drive is flat, water will sit, freeze, and break down the surface—especially in shoulder seasons where daytime thaws turn to overnight ice. When planning a new install or replacement (which might cost $3,400–$8,800 for asphalt), make sure your quote includes site grading and clear direction for runoff. Never let any contractor sell you on “level” unless they mean perpendicular to the drive, not along its length.

Does sealcoating fix cracks?

Sealcoating in Tunkhannock is good preventive maintenance, but it’s not a miracle cure for cracks. What sealcoating does is slow down oxidation, keeping the surface from drying and getting brittle under sun and salt. For cracks under a quarter inch wide, a proper crack fill before sealcoating does extend life and improve appearance. But if you try to sealcoat over larger, unfilled cracks or structural failures, you’re just hiding the problem for a few months. Eventually, water gets in, freezes, and makes things worse. Sealcoating jobs typically run $200–$700 and are best used on drives with good base and only minor surface aging. For any serious cracking or base damage, save your money on sealant and focus on proper repairs first.
