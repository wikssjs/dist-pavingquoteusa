What a new driveway costs in Cambria County

In Cambria County, where about 72.5% of homes are single-family and homeownership rates approach 15,109 households (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), most driveway projects are for owners, not landlords. Driveway replacement is a real investment here, as the combination of heavy snow loads, deep winter frost, and high rates of pre-1980 housing mean old driveways have often seen decades of freeze-thaw cycles, shifting subgrade, and patchwork repairs. The price for a new driveway is influenced more by what goes under the surface — like the depth of excavation, thickness of crushed rock, and drainage details — than the visible asphalt or concrete. Especially in Cambria Township, with its shallow shale and rocky soils, skimping on the unseen base almost guarantees surface cracking or heave after a winter or two. Don’t assume two quotes for “asphalt” or “concrete” mean the same work underneath; the differences that matter most are out of sight.

The honest case for and against sealcoating

Sealcoating in Cambria County is widely promoted — and widely misunderstood. Properly applied, a sealcoat protects your asphalt from oxidizing in the sun, shields it against dripped oil and fuel, and gives a clean, uniform black finish. This is helpful, especially where curb appeal matters, or where road salt routinely gets tracked up the drive. But sealcoating does not “renew” a driveway that’s failing from below, nor does it add structural strength. If your asphalt is already showing deep cracks, sinking, or base movement, no amount of sealant will fix the underlying problem.

One thing almost no one selling sealcoating will tell you: sealing too often actually harms your driveway. Repeated, thick layers can make the surface brittle and prone to peeling. Worse, spreading sealer on new asphalt (less than 6–12 months old) traps uncured volatiles, which weakens the paved surface. For Cambria County, every 3–4 years is plenty unless high traffic or heavy contamination demands more.

The part that matters more than sealing is crack filling — done with a hot-applied rubber or asphalt-based compound, which blocks water from getting down to the base and causing freeze-thaw expansion. A rushed or cheap sealcoat, especially sprayed thin from an unmarked pickup, is cosmetic only and may wash off in one winter. A proper job uses a squeegee or broom to force sealer into pores and joints, not just mist it on.

Most driveways in the county are suited to a $200–$650 job every few years. If you’re quoted far less, expect little more than dyed water and a few weeks of black shine.

Door-knocking crews and leftover material

Every paving season, transient crews work Cambria County — especially in the more rural northern townships, where neighborhoods are spread out and local verification isn’t as quick. The classic pitch is “leftover asphalt from a job down the road” or a time-limited offer to “save on trucking.” You’ll see unmarked trucks, sometimes with out-of-county plates, and a demand for cash or a verbal quote with no paperwork.

These operators count on the fact that defective paving may look fine for a year or two, but may be only a half-inch thick or laid on an unprepared base. Thin oil-based sprays passed off as “sealcoating” only disguise more serious problems for a season. Today-only pricing is a pressure tactic: reputable contractors in Cambria County do not knock doors or need fast decisions, and they put all terms in writing.

Under Pennsylvania’s Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act (HICPA), any home improvement job over $500 must be put in a written contract. You also have an unconditional right to cancel for three business days. No contractor can legally offer or encourage you to waive your homeowner’s insurance deductible, and demanding it is a red flag.

Here’s what stops every scam: verify HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, check workers’ compensation status through WCAIS, and never sign or pay until you’ve seen a written scope, address, and references. Five minutes with these tools will protect your entire driveway budget.

Comparing paving estimates in Cambria County

The real work in paving isn’t picking the cheapest quote — it’s understanding what each bid actually covers. In Cambria County, two estimates for “asphalt driveway replacement” can look similar but vary by thousands, mostly because one spells out excavation and base details, and the other is vague. Always look for the measured square footage, the planned removal depth, the type and thickness of stone base, method and number of compaction passes (in lifts), the thickness of each asphalt layer (not just “3 inches,” but compacted depth), where edges will be finished, and how water will be managed. Warranty and payment schedule count, too. Most warranties exclude cracks, because freeze-thaw cycles and base movement are nearly inevitable here.

If you get a one-line total with no breakdown, you’re being asked to buy a pig in a poke. A full, written estimate is not just for show — it’s how you compare apples to apples and reveal built-in shortcuts before they become expensive repairs.

Permits and approvals for paving in Cambria County

In most of Cambria County, including Adams Township, repaving an existing driveway—so long as the dimensions and drainage don’t change—generally does not require a specific driveway construction permit. This is a relief for many, saving time and paperwork. However, if you are in Johnstown and plan to expand or alter your paved driveway, especially if you’re changing curb cuts or affecting stormwater flow, you’ll likely need zoning or building permit review from the City of Johnstown Codes Department. If your project involves connecting or widening a driveway on a state-maintained road, you must secure a driveway approach permit through PennDOT District 10. Township and borough roads use their own permit process, so always check the local office if you’re making changes to where your drive meets the road.

Increasing the paved (impervious) area can also trigger stormwater runoff requirements, both for code and for neighbor relations. Directing drainage onto a neighbor’s property is never legal, regardless of permit status, and can lead to claims or required corrections.

Before hiring, verify a contractor’s Home Improvement Contractor registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, and confirm that their workers’ compensation policy is current through the WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). For pre-1978 Johnstown homes, EPA lead certification is another must-check for any disturbance that could affect existing paint.

The specifications to demand in writing

If you take nothing else from this page, take this: you need measurements, not promises, on your contract. In Cambria County’s climate, with 40 psf snow loads and a 36-inch frost depth, a shallow or unclear base will not survive more than a few winters, especially over the rocky, poorly draining soils in areas like Cambria Township. The difference between jobs that last and those that don’t isn’t visible after the asphalt goes down — it’s in the thickness and quality of the layers you can’t see. “We’ll put down three inches” means little unless it specifies compacted thickness (because hot asphalt can lose a quarter of its depth when compacted), and where the inches are measured.

Insist that every critical layer is specified in writing, with material, thickness, compaction method, and supplier if you want to compare apples to apples. It might feel awkward to ask, but it’s normal in this business, and any contractor who balks is gambling on you not knowing the difference.

  • Total measured square footage to be paved
  • Depth and method of removal/excavation
  • Type and depth (inches, compacted) of aggregate base
  • Compaction method and lift thickness for each layer
  • Binder course asphalt mix and compacted thickness
  • Surface (top) course mix and compacted thickness
  • How edges are treated (tapered, set flush, supported)
  • Slope and drainage direction for water runoff
  • Name or grade of asphalt/concrete mix used
  • Cleanup, topsoil restoration, and joint to street or sidewalk

Getting thirty years out of a driveway

In Cambria County, driveway longevity begins and ends with water control. Filling cracks as soon as they form is the single most important step—blocking water from reaching the base stops freeze-thaw cycles from opening those cracks into full-fledged potholes. Next is keeping the edges supported: overhanging or unsupported edges crumble when driven over or exposed to plows.

Always direct water off the surface and away from your home and driveway edges, using slope or perimeter drains as needed. Never allow water to stand or flow across the surface, especially in late winter, when nighttime freezing shoves up the pavement. Don’t store point loads (like dumpsters or jack stands) on the drive—these “hot spots” will settle faster than the rest.

For new asphalt, allow at least 72 hours before parking and a month before turning tired wheels when stopped. On concrete, de-icing salt is dangerous: it accelerates spalling and can cut the lifespan by half, so stick to sand, calcium magnesium acetate, or granular clay when possible. On asphalt, rock salt is less damaging to the material but does encourage more freeze/thaw cycling, which remains the main enemy in local conditions.

Finding a legitimate paver in Cambria County

Legitimate contractors in Cambria County are not hard to find if you use the right checks. Start by verifying Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Ask for a local address, not just a cell number; dodging this is a major red flag. Rented or out-of-area equipment is another concern—reputable outfits own or lease locally because they run crews through most of the season. References from two or three years ago are worth more than a stack of recent jobs; most defects in driveways surface after a few freeze-thaw cycles, not in the first summer. Every credible paving contractor in this area knows where their asphalt mix comes from — usually a plant in Johnstown or Ebensburg. If they can’t answer, you’re dealing with a broker, not a contractor.

If a crew can’t answer these, or rushes you to sign, walk away. Most problems can be spotted before you sign, not after the drive is down.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Cambria County

Is an overlay worth it or should I start over? An overlay (resurfacing) in Cambria County is appropriate when the driveway’s base is solid and hasn’t shifted, but the surface shows cracks or minor pitting. Resurfacing costs $1,450–$4,300. It gives you 8–15 years if done over a strong base. However, if your driveway has heaved, sunk, or shows spiderweb cracks deep into the surface, the problem usually starts below—patching on top just hides the issue for a few years, never fixes it. In Johnstown and the northern townships, where freeze-thaw is roughest, starting over (full replacement) is the only move that prevents money lost on repeated overlays. Get a contractor to core or dig a test spot if you’re not sure.
Why are my driveway edges crumbling? Edge crumbling is common in Cambria County’s older driveways, especially where snow plows and traffic repeatedly overload unsupported asphalt or concrete. The subgrade here—often rocky or shallow shale in Cambria Township—doesn’t provide much lateral support. Without a beefed-up, compacted aggregate base at the edges, or if topsoil is allowed to erode away, the sides inevitably crack and break off. You can have a good surface but still lose edge chunks every winter; it’s a sign to add support before your next full replacement or overlay, not just patch it each season. For older Johnstown mill-era homes with narrow drives, consider a concrete curb or reinforce the edge when you repave.
What is the difference between binder and surface course? In Cambria County, a proper asphalt driveway is built in at least two lifts: the binder course and the surface (top) course. The binder is the bottom, coarser asphalt layer — it provides bulk strength and helps bridge minor subgrade shifts. The surface course is finer, using smaller aggregate for a smoother finish and better resistance to water and wear. Specifying both in your quote is vital here because the binder handles the freeze-thaw stress from below, while the surface course protects against traffic, salt, and weather. Some low-end jobbers skip a true binder course, which shaves cost but shortens the lifespan dramatically. It takes both, each compacted to proper thickness, if you want 20–30 years out of your investment in a $3,300–$8,600 replacement.
Do I need an approach permit where my driveway meets the road? Yes, for any new or widened connection to a public roadway in Cambria County, you need an “approach” permit at the point your driveway meets the street. On state highways, that permit comes from PennDOT District 10. On township or borough roads, it’s managed by the local office. In Johnstown, curb cut or new connection work is reviewed by the codes department. Repaving a like-for-like driveway doesn’t require a permit in places like Adams Township, but as soon as you expand or alter curb access, permitting is mandatory. Ignoring this can delay completion or force rework — always ask before the crew arrives, especially on older streets or when modifying drainage.
What kind of soil is under Cambria County driveways? Cambria County’s soil is a mixed bag. In Cambria Township, shallow shale and fractured rock are common, needing thicker, well-compacted aggregate bases to prevent frost heave. In Johnstown, some lots have fill or reworked soils from house construction a century ago, which means variable support and sometimes trapped moisture. Wherever you are, deep winter frost (36 in) and heavy freeze-thaw cycles make a properly prepared base non-negotiable. If a contractor tells you a thinner base is okay “for this soil,” they don’t know the county. It’s the reason a full replacement costs $3,300–$8,600—and why those who cut corners see cracks early.
What slope does a driveway need for drainage? For Cambria County’s climate, a residential driveway should slope away from the house at a minimum of 1/4 inch per foot (2%), which keeps water moving off the surface and out to the street, ditch, or rain garden. Flat driveways or those sloping toward the home nearly guarantee frost heave, ice dams, and eventual water infiltration. In older Johnstown neighborhoods, where grades can be tough, a well-designed swale or trench drain may be required to meet permitting or just to protect your basement. Always get the slope and drainage direction stated in your contract, as it’s buried details like this—not just price—that decide whether you get 30 years or five out of your $4,800–$11,500 investment.
Is sealcoating a scam? Sealcoating isn’t a scam—but it’s often oversold in Cambria County and all over Pennsylvania. On a sound driveway, it’s minor maintenance: it blocks UV, protects against oil, and looks better, usually for $200–$650. But it’s not structural, and no sealer will rescue a cracked or failed base. “Annual” sealcoating is a marketing tactic; too-frequent or the wrong product can actually shorten a driveway’s life by building up brittle layers. The real scam is thin, spray-on jobs from door-knockers who don’t prep or clean the surface, and who may leave you with nothing more than a dirty shine. Done every few years, after proper cleaning and crack filling, sealcoating is safe and might extend a newer drive’s surface appearance—but it’s rarely worth the hype or urgent pitches.
How much does a concrete driveway cost in Cambria County? A new concrete driveway in Cambria County will run $4,800–$11,500 depending on size, thickness, and site grading. This price reflects both the heavy frost and rough subgrade common here—concrete needs a thicker gravel base and durable mix to handle winter. Upfront cost is higher than asphalt, but with a 30–50 year lifespan and less frequent maintenance, concrete can be a smart tradeoff in places where plow damage and salt use aren’t extreme. If your goal is lowest life-cycle cost, or if you want a surface less likely to soften in summer sun or rut under heavy trucks, concrete is worth a look—but be sure your contractor knows how to install for deep frost and cures the slab before any de-icing salts go down.
What does sealcoating cost in Cambria County? For an average Cambria County residential driveway, you’ll pay $200–$650 for a single professional sealcoating application. Larger or wide drives in Richland and upper Westmont may land at the top of that range, but small city lots in Johnstown’s West End will be lower. Be wary of quotes under $200; that usually means a thin, spray-only application that won’t last through the winter. The key cost drivers are surface area, whether cracks need filling (which is sometimes extra), and access. Many firms offer package pricing for annual returning customers, but a sound driveway only needs sealing every 3–4 years at most—even less often if not heavily trafficked.
How long before I can drive on a new asphalt driveway? In Cambria County’s cooler climate, a new asphalt driveway generally needs about 72 hours (3 days) before you drive or park on it. During hot spells, or if the surface still feels tender, waiting 4–5 days is safer. Even after parking is allowed, avoid turning your wheels when stopped—this can scar or rut the surface. Full curing (when sealer can be safely applied, or when heavy loads like dumpsters should be parked) takes several months, ideally the first winter. Early use and sharp turns do the most damage in the first week. Installers will flag the drive or give you a definite timeline—don’t let anyone rush you, even if rain or schedules press.
How do I verify a paving contractor in Pennsylvania? Every legitimate paving contractor working in Cambria County must have a valid Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration if the job is over $500—check it yourself at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, searching the business name or registration number. Also confirm their workers’ compensation coverage is active at wcais.pa.gov. Ask for a written contract that references the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, with your cancellation rights spelled out. Check references from at least 2–3 years ago, visit local jobs, and ask which Johnstown or Ebensburg plant supplies their mix. These quick steps, before you sign or pay, will protect you from the worst scams and save you a possible $5,300 disappointment.
