Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors generally must be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office if the job exceeds $500. Before work begins, verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Also be cautious of “storm chasers” who solicit driveway work door-to-door after bad weather; if they can’t provide a verifiable PA HIC registration, that’s a significant red flag.

Permits: most residential driveway paving usually does not require a building permit inside the property line, but the driveway approach—where your driveway meets the public road—may require approvals. In practice, that can mean a driveway access permit from your municipality and/or PennDOT review depending on the location and any regrading near the road. Since requirements vary by municipality, check before excavation.

Runoff control: Pennsylvania stormwater regulations (Chapter 102) require that paving work does not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. Contractors should maintain proper slope and may need drainage improvements if your driveway currently directs water incorrectly.

Workers’ compensation matters too. Verify workers’ comp coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can expose you to liability if someone is injured on your site.

Step-by-step in South Hanover: (1) look up HIC registration online at the Attorney General site; (2) ask for a current Certificate of Insurance and confirm general liability limits; (3) verify workers’ comp coverage at wcais.pa.gov; then (4) confirm any required driveway/curb-cut approvals with your municipality before base removal or regrading.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your South Hanover driveway?

For South Hanover homeowners, the freeze-thaw cycle is the defining factor in the asphalt vs. concrete decision. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt tends to be more forgiving under repeated expansion because it is flexible, which helps it resist brittle cracking. Concrete can perform well for decades, but in Dauphin County winters it may spall or crack when road salt and repeated freeze-thaw cycles take their toll—especially if the subbase is weak or the slab lacks proper drainage.

Home condition drives the decision as much as material choice. About 36% of the local housing stock is pre-1980, and that age often means existing driveways are near or past expected service life. In those situations, resurfacing or sealcoating usually cannot correct an underlying base that has already shifted. That’s why many homeowners end up choosing full asphalt replacement—often in the $7,000–$15,000 range—rather than trying to push life out of a failing system.

Here’s a practical dollar example: if a driveway has surface cracking only and the base is solid, an overlay may land in the $3,500–$9,000 band. But if the driveway is alligator cracking with settled areas near the garage, overlaying over compromised base is likely to shorten the life of the repair and can cost more overall when you redo it sooner.

Bottom line for South Hanover: choose asphalt when your goal is flexibility through Penn’s winter cycles and maintenance planning; choose concrete when you want a long-term, lower-maintenance surface and you can invest in proper base preparation and drainage from day one.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in South Hanover

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in South Hanover?

In South Hanover and across Dauphin County, sealcoating works best as preventive maintenance—usually every 2–3 years once the asphalt is properly cured. If your driveway is newly paved, wait through the full cure period recommended by your contractor before applying sealcoat. If you already have a stable surface (no ongoing settlement, no deep potholes, and cracks that are mostly cosmetic), sealcoating helps slow oxidation and reduces water penetration. Keep in mind sealcoating is not a structural fix: if you’re seeing alligator cracking, repeated patch failures, or areas that sink after frost, you typically need base repair and asphalt replacement instead of a surface coat. Budget-wise, standard sealcoating commonly falls in the $300–$900 range for a typical residential driveway depending on square footage and prep requirements. For best results in Pennsylvania’s freeze-thaw environment, make sure crack filling is handled before sealcoat and that drainage issues are addressed—otherwise water will keep finding its way under the pavement.

What causes driveway cracks in South Hanover?

Driveway cracks in South Hanover are usually a combination of movement and water. The biggest drivers locally are freeze-thaw cycles, trapped moisture, and older base conditions that can’t handle repeated expansion and contraction. Common patterns include hairline cracking from thermal stress, wider cracks that open in spring, and alligator cracking that indicates fatigue from weak pavement or a base that’s breaking down. Settlement near garages or where the driveway transitions to concrete often signals inadequate base support or improper grading so water collects. If your driveway was installed before modern base and drainage standards—something that’s more likely given the area’s older housing stock (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—cracks can show up sooner and grow faster. Simple crack filling can help for minor cracks under about 1/4 inch, especially if the surface is otherwise stable, but it won’t correct a failing base. In those cases, full replacement is typically the durable answer, while resurfacing/overlay is best when cracking is mostly superficial.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in South Hanover?

Coverage varies widely based on your policy and the cause of the damage. In South Hanover, homeowners often ask after storm events—like a sudden washout from runoff, a fallen tree, or impact damage. Many policies cover sudden and accidental damage, but routine deterioration (age-related cracking, settlement over years, or general wear from freeze-thaw) is usually not covered. It’s also important to distinguish between cosmetic cracking and structural failure: insurers may require photos and documentation that show the specific event and date of damage. If water intrusion or drainage problems are contributing, you may need to prove causation. As a practical note, if damage is from normal aging, you’ll usually be looking at maintenance or replacement decisions; for asphalt, that can mean sealcoating in the $300–$900 band for preventive work, or a full replacement in the $7,000–$15,000 range when the base is compromised. The best approach is to contact your insurance carrier promptly, provide documentation, and have a paving contractor inspect and describe the condition and likely cause in plain terms.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

For Pennsylvania residential driveways, the “right” asphalt thickness depends on whether you’re doing a full installation, an overlay, and how solid your existing base is. A durable approach in Dauphin County typically uses a compacted stone base and then asphalt placed in two lifts so it compacts evenly and resists the freeze-thaw cycle. As a rule of thumb, overlay thickness is not the same as full-depth installation: an overlay may be around 2 inches when the base is sound, while full-depth work commonly requires about 3–4 inches of asphalt total to support the pavement through heaving cycles and repeated winter temperature changes. That thickness guidance matters because a thin top can look fine initially but fail sooner if the base is weak or drainage is off. The best way to confirm for your exact driveway is an onsite evaluation—especially if you’re dealing with settled areas, potholes, or alligator cracking, which often point to base rebuilding rather than simply adding more asphalt.

How long does driveway paving take in South Hanover?

The timeline for paving a driveway in South Hanover depends on scope: sealcoating is typically a faster, same-week type of job, while full replacement takes longer because it includes excavation, base preparation, compaction, asphalt placement in lifts, and careful curing time. Scheduling also depends on seasonal lead times—around the Harrisburg area, local contractor competition can stretch lead times into 4–8 weeks during peak periods. Once crews arrive on-site, many full asphalt driveways are completed in about 1–3 days of active work, but the overall project also includes access coordination, layout, and weather buffering. Concrete driveways usually require more curing considerations. Your contractor should provide a written arrival estimate and completion window, and discuss rain/freeze delays before starting. If your driveway needs regrading for drainage, expect additional time for prep and base corrections before asphalt or concrete is placed.

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in South Hanover?

Failing base conditions in South Hanover often show up as repeated surface distress in specific areas. Look for settled or sunken spots that don’t recover, potholes that keep returning, edges that drop or crack away from the garage, and patterns like alligator cracking that spread across panels—especially when the cracking is paired with rutting or visible voids. If you notice water pooling after rain, base failure is more likely because trapped moisture expands and contracts during Pennsylvania freeze-thaw cycles. Another sign is “patchwork that never lasts,” where previous repairs break down quickly because the problem is below the surface. In these cases, crack filling or sealcoating is usually treating symptoms. While crack filling and patching can be a short-term fix in the $200–$800 band for minor cracks, a failing base typically calls for base rebuild and asphalt full replacement (often $7,000–$15,000 depending on size and excavation depth). The most reliable decision comes from an evaluation that confirms base condition before recommending overlay vs. replacement.
