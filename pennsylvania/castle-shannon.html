Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors are commonly required to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office if the work exceeds $500. Before you sign anything, verify the contractor at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Also watch for “storm chasers”—anyone who solicits driveway work door-to-door after a storm without verifiable PA HIC registration should be treated as a major red flag.

Permits: residential driveway paving typically does not require a building permit in many PA municipalities, but the driveway approach—where the driveway meets the public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. This is especially relevant when changing the slope near the road, adjusting tie-ins, or altering drainage that affects the right-of-way. Check before excavation so you’re not forced to redo work.

Water runoff matters. Under Pennsylvania stormwater requirements (commonly referred to by Chapter 102), paving projects generally can’t increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. That means grading and drainage solutions may be part of your scope, not “extras.”

Workers’ compensation is another must-check. Confirm coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can expose you to liability if a worker is hurt on your property.

Step-by-step for Castle Shannon homeowners: (1) verify HIC registration online; (2) request a certificate of insurance showing general liability; (3) request proof of workers’ compensation coverage; (4) make sure the driveway access permit need is confirmed in writing if your work touches the road/sidewalk area; and (5) keep copies of all documents with your contract and invoice.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Castle Shannon driveway?

In Castle Shannon, the freeze–thaw cycle is the deciding factor. Asphalt and concrete both expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, which helps it better tolerate winter movement; however, it needs preventive maintenance like sealcoating every 2–3 years to slow oxidation and protect the surface from salt and water intrusion. Concrete is rigid; it can last a long time, but it’s more prone to spalling or cracking when road salt, moisture, and freeze–thaw stress concentrate—particularly if the base isn’t properly prepared or if deicers are used heavily.

Because 82.8% of Castle Shannon homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many existing driveways are near or beyond typical service expectations. That’s why full replacement is often recommended more than resurfacing. If the base has shifted or water ponds, resurfacing (often $2,500–$6,500) may only be a temporary improvement; a full-depth asphalt replacement (often $4,500–$11,000) is usually the longer-term fix.

A concrete option can be justified when you want a low-maintenance surface and are willing to invest upfront—especially if the contractor designs the section for the soil and drainage conditions. For example, if asphalt quotes land around $6,200 for a full replacement but the concrete quote is around $10,500, that $4,300 difference can be worth it when you plan to stay in the home and want fewer future resurfacing cycles, provided the base is engineered and the drainage is corrected.

Bottom line: choose asphalt for flexibility and cost-effectiveness with proper maintenance; choose concrete for long-term durability when the base and drainage are done right. In most older Allegheny County driveways, the “right” choice starts with base condition—not just the surface material.

Should I repair or replace my driveway in Castle Shannon?

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Castle Shannon?

Sealcoating is typically scheduled every 2–3 years when the asphalt surface is still structurally sound—meaning minimal rutting, no major base movement, and cracking that hasn’t progressed into widespread fatigue. In Castle Shannon, the goal is to slow oxidation, reduce water intrusion, and protect the binder from salt and freeze–thaw wear. If your driveway is already showing deep cracks, alligator cracking, or areas where the pavement has settled, sealcoating alone won’t fix the underlying problem—an overlay or replacement is usually more cost-effective long-term. What causes driveway cracks in Castle Shannon?

Driveway cracks in Castle Shannon are most often caused by a combination of moisture intrusion, base movement, and Pennsylvania temperature cycling. Freeze–thaw cycles expand water in soil and base stone, which can push pavement upward and lead to cracking patterns like longitudinal and alligator cracking. Drainage issues are a frequent contributor—if the driveway slope is too flat or the edges aren’t retaining material, water can collect and work its way under the asphalt. In older Allegheny County properties, utility cut patches can also create weak spots that expand differently than the surrounding pavement. Over time, traffic loads compound small failures, turning them into larger cracks. Road salt and the natural aging of asphalt binder can further accelerate surface cracking. The best prevention is correct base prep, proper grading so water flows away, and timely preventive maintenance like sealcoating when the pavement is still sound.
