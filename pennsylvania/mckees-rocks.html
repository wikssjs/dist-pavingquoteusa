Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, legitimate driveway paving contractors typically need to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office when the job exceeds $500. You can verify a contractor’s registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be especially careful of “storm chaser” crews after heavy weather: soliciting driveway work door-to-door is a major red flag if they can’t provide clear, verifiable PA HIC registration.

Permits are often the confusing part. In many PA municipalities, residential driveway paving doesn’t require a building permit for the driveway itself, but the driveway approach where it meets the public road may require a driveway access permit through PennDOT or your local municipality. Check before excavation—especially if you’re changing slopes, widening the apron, or connecting to curb/sidewalk infrastructure.

Runoff matters under Pennsylvania stormwater rules (Chapter 102). Your project shouldn’t increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way, so grading and drainage solutions may be part of compliance.

Also verify workers’ compensation. Confirm coverage at wcais.pa.gov. An uninsured crew can expose you to liability on your property.

How to verify step-by-step in McKees Rocks: (1) check PA HIC registration online; (2) ask for an up-to-date certificate of insurance (general liability plus auto if they haul equipment); (3) request proof of workers’ compensation coverage; (4) confirm whether a driveway access permit is required before work begins and ensure the crew follows Chapter 102 runoff expectations.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your McKees Rocks driveway?

For McKees Rocks homeowners, the freeze–thaw cycle is the deciding factor when choosing asphalt versus concrete. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, so it typically handles freeze–thaw cracking better—provided the driveway has a properly prepared base and you keep up preventive maintenance like sealcoating every 2–3 years. Concrete is rigid and durable, but heavy winter cycling and road salt can lead to surface spalling or cracking over time, particularly if drainage is poor or joints were not detailed correctly.

Local housing stock also pushes the decision toward the “right rehab.” With 88.4% of homes built before 1980, many driveways are already at or beyond expected wear. That often means full replacement is more common than overlaying, because the base may have softened from decades of water infiltration, utility cuts, and drainage issues. If you only resurface a driveway with hidden base problems, the new pavement can start cracking sooner than expected.

A concrete example: if your asphalt driveway is structurally failing and needs full-depth work, asphalt replacement might land in the $4,500–$11,000 band, while a new concrete driveway commonly falls in the $7,000–$16,000 range. The difference can be justified when you want longer-term, low-maintenance use and you’re comfortable managing concrete’s risks (good drainage, appropriate jointing, and responsible de-icing). On the other hand, if your base is sound and your damage is mostly surface-level, asphalt resurfacing in the $2,500–$6,500 range is often the smarter value—because you’re spending to correct the top layer, not rebuilding the foundation.

How long does a new asphalt driveway last in McKees Rocks's climate?

A new asphalt driveway in McKees Rocks typically lasts about 20–30 years when the base is built correctly and drainage is handled. In Allegheny County, freeze–thaw cycles and winter salt don’t forgive thin sections or weak subbase, so the lifespan depends heavily on base prep depth and proper compaction. Should I repair or replace my driveway in McKees Rocks?

Repair vs. replacement comes down to what’s happening underneath the surface. In McKees Rocks, you can often repair small issues if cracking is minor and the driveway isn’t holding water, but structural signs usually point to replacement. Examples of “repair may be enough” include a few cracks under about 1/4 inch or localized patch failures; those small jobs can sometimes be handled with crack filling and patching. If you’re seeing widespread fatigue cracking, rutting, or areas that soften or pump underfoot, that’s a base-prep red flag—an overlay or full replacement is usually the better long-term move. Overlay/resurfacing in the $2,500–$6,500 range can make sense when the base is truly solid. When the base fails or drainage is causing ponding through Pennsylvania’s freeze–thaw cycle, full replacement in the $4,500–$11,000 band is often the most cost-effective option over time.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in McKees Rocks?

Sealcoating is best considered preventive maintenance—typically every 2–3 years—when your asphalt is still structurally sound. In McKees Rocks, the freeze–thaw cycle and road salt accelerate asphalt aging, so waiting too long can turn surface oxidation into bigger cracks and deeper deterioration. If your pavement has tight surface cracking, no major rutting, and no evidence of base movement, sealcoating can help extend the service life. The usual sealcoating budget for a standard driveway often lands in the $250–$800 range per application depending on square footage and condition. If the driveway has widespread fatigue cracking, loose aggregate, or ponding that suggests drainage/base trouble, sealcoating won’t solve the root cause—an overlay or full replacement is more appropriate. Your contractor should also confirm that the weather and pavement moisture conditions are right for curing before they apply sealant.

What causes driveway cracks in McKees Rocks?

Driveway cracks in McKees Rocks are usually caused by a mix of base and weather-related stress. Pennsylvania freeze–thaw cycles are a major factor: when water infiltrates through cracks, it freezes, expands, and lifts pavement, leading to alligator cracking and spalling. Poor drainage and low spots can speed this up because ponded water keeps the subbase saturated. In older Allegheny County neighborhoods, utility cuts and settlement also contribute—one patch can change how stress transfers across the driveway. Additionally, if the asphalt was installed too thin for local winter performance or if the base wasn’t properly compacted, minor movement becomes cracking over time. If you’re seeing fine hairline cracks only, sealcoating and crack filling may slow further deterioration. If you’re seeing interconnected cracking, rutting, or movement, the real fix is base and grading correction—not just surface patching.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in McKees Rocks?

It can, but it depends on the cause of the damage and the specific terms of your policy. Most homeowner’s insurance is designed for sudden, accidental events (like a vehicle impact or certain storm-related damage), not for wear and tear from normal aging or freeze–thaw deterioration. In McKees Rocks and across Pennsylvania, asphalt and concrete can degrade over time due to temperature cycling and salt exposure, which is typically considered maintenance-related. If the damage follows a covered event—such as a tree falling, an unexpected sinkhole-related event (rare), or certain storm impacts—insurance may cover repairs, but you’ll still need documentation. Ask your paving contractor to provide clear photos and pre/post condition notes for your claim. For budgeting, many insurance-related repairs start with patching or crack filling, while full replacement is often in the $4,500–$11,000 range for asphalt depending on scope and base condition. Always confirm coverage with your insurer, and don’t sign away rights or lock into a repair plan without understanding what the policy requires.
