Driveway permits, approaches and stormwater rules

In Northampton County, most Lehigh homeowners will not need a building permit to repave a driveway in its current footprint. For example, in Palmer Township, minor asphalt patching can be completed without permits. However, if you plan to expand your driveway or install a new one, areas such as Bethlehem require a zoning or building permit review, so check before you start any enlargement projects.

The driveway approach—where your drive meets a public or state road—is handled separately. Most homeowners assume the township handles this, but if your driveway connects to a state route, the permit comes from PennDOT District 5-0; local township offices handle curb cuts for local streets. Getting this step wrong can create problems with access or future property inspections.

If you increase the impervious area by widening your driveway, expect that stormwater management requirements may be triggered. You are never permitted to direct runoff onto your neighbor’s property, regardless of permits. Improper drainage can cause legal headaches, especially with Northampton County’s limestone subgrade leading to settlement or water movement issues.

When choosing a contractor, Pennsylvania requires Home Improvement Contractor registration for any driveway contract over $500. You can verify registrations at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. All employers, including paving crews, must carry workers' compensation insurance, which you can check at the state’s online WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). Never skip these steps: they are your best protections.

Comparing paving estimates in Lehigh

Not all driveway estimates are created equal. If you can't compare what's included, you have no way to know what you're buying. Every Lehigh quote should list the square footage measured on site, the proposed excavation and how spoils are disposed of, the type and thickness of aggregate base, how each asphalt course will be installed (including compacted depth), drainage details, and what will be done along the edges. Restoration of lawn edges and disturbed areas is a real cost in a neighborhood of mature landscaping.

The payment schedule should be reasonable: a modest deposit with balance due only upon completion. Warranty coverage in paving contracts tends to exclude cracking—which, ironically, is what most people care about. Look for warranties that clearly define what is and isn't covered.

If you receive an estimate that’s just a lump sum with no specs or line items—a single most common trick in Lehigh Valley’s saturated market—you can’t meaningfully compare it to anything else. That’s how driveways with thin bases and poor compaction get sold at market rates and fail much sooner than they should.

Diagnosing a failing Lehigh driveway

The first and most important step in fixing a Lehigh driveway is diagnosing whether the base has failed. Here’s your self-diagnosis: Look for alligator cracking — a web of cracks stretching over wide areas. This means the base is shot, and any overlay is doomed to fail. Isolated, straight cracks suggest surface aging, and crack filling plus sealcoating can buy years. Depressions that hold water (“birdbaths”) tell you the subgrade has moved, likely due to water or freeze-thaw heave. Edges that crumble away show lack of support, a classic problem with older Northamption County installs. Potholes mean water has punched through the surface — by then, the base is holding water, not just the surface.

Do not let any contractor sell you an overlay or patch over a failing base. If they don’t probe or core the base, they’re guessing at your expense. It’s better to pay for a few square feet of proper replacement than to have the whole job fail early.

Caring for a new Lehigh driveway

Driveway longevity in Lehigh comes down to doing the simple maintenance before small problems become big ones. First: fill any cracks as soon as you see them, before water can seep down to the base and freeze. Edge support is next; don’t let vehicles drive off the edge repeatedly, and use clean-up stone or curbing to prevent the asphalt or concrete undermining. Always keep surface water moving away from the driveway—and the house—using swales or drains. Don’t park dumpsters or use jack stands directly on the surface for extended periods, which cause costly impressions in both asphalt and concrete.

If you’ve just had new asphalt installed, stay off it for at least 3–7 days (longer in cool, wet weather) and avoid turning the wheels sharply when parked for the first few weeks. Let it cure before what’s called a “dry steer.”

In Lehigh Valley winters, salt should be used sparingly on concrete—frequent freeze-thaw cycles (with winter lows down to 4 °F and a 36 inch frost depth) mean deicers can damage concrete quickly. On asphalt, the freeze-thaw cycling caused by deicing is the culprit, not the salt itself; sand or calcium magnesium acetate are alternatives, but they cost more and aren’t easy to find locally in bulk.

How often should a Lehigh driveway be sealed?

Sealcoating gets a lot of attention, but it’s not a fix-all. A good sealcoat shields the asphalt binder from the sun (oxidation), fuel drips, and household oils. It also makes the surface look sharp for a couple of years. What it doesn't do: increase the driveway’s structural strength, stop water from entering structural cracks, or resurrect a failing base. Used properly, sealcoating extends the driveway’s life by preserving flexibility in the top surface — but only if the base is solid.

Seal too often and layers build up, leading to peeling and ugly cracks. Fresh asphalt needs to cure for months before its first sealcoat; sealing too soon can lock in volatiles and soften your driveway. In Lehigh, a fair schedule is every two to four years, not yearly, and only after the surface passes the “water droplet test”— when water no longer beads but soaks in.

The most neglected driveway maintenance is actually crack filling, which stops water before it can undermine the base. Hire for crack fill and proper squeegee or brush-applied sealcoat — not just a sprayed-on cheap job. When you see prices well under $200–$700 for a typical Lehigh driveway, it’s usually a thin spray that won’t last a full season and may even accelerate peeling and cracking.

Local soil, drainage and driveway lifespan

The Lehigh Valley’s limestone karst subgrade is unlike anywhere else in the state and directly impacts how a driveway should be built. Limestone karst is full of channels and voids, which allow water to move underground and can cause uneven settlement if the base isn't engineered for it. Compared to counties sitting on clay or shale, Northampton requires extra care in base grading and drainage—water can pool in layers under the driveway and lead to sudden dips or movement after a hard winter.

The local frost depth is 36 inches due to brutal freeze-thaw cycles, and Lehigh’s driveways need to be built with deeper, well-drained bases than the older installations typical of Easton and Bethlehem (with their many houses from before 1940). A driveway laid decades ago likely sits on a shallow or mixed base—often just six inches or less of whatever stone was close at hand—which cannot withstand the cycles of freezing and thawing today’s code anticipates. Modern standards call for more excavation and thoughtful grading to keep water away from the slab or pavement, especially where dense older neighborhoods might still have original driveways. For Lehigh, that’s not optional—it’s the difference between a driveway that lasts and one that crumbles.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Lehigh

What does an asphalt overlay cost in Lehigh? The cost of an asphalt overlay in Lehigh depends on the square footage and that the existing base is still solid. For a typical single-family drive, homeowners should expect $1,500–$4,500 in 2026. Overlay is feasible only if your base hasn’t failed—meaning no widespread alligator cracking or deep depressions. This is common in Lehigh where older driveways may look rough but still have a sound foundation. It’s a cost-effective option if the underlying stone is in good shape but should never be used to mask structural problems. Always ask the contractor how they’re verifying the base’s soundness before signing an overlay contract.
How much does driveway paving cost in Lehigh? For a standard full replacement asphalt driveway in Lehigh (with a new base), current estimates for 2026 run $3,500–$9,000. Most typical homeowners in Lehigh, with lots averaging in the 50–100 foot range, will land somewhere around the middle of that band. These costs reflect the need for deep excavation and new base stone due to Lehigh’s limestone karst soil. Shorter drives or those that can reuse substantial sections of base may be at the lower end, while long or unusually wide driveways are at the top.
How deep should the aggregate base be here? For Lehigh driveways, base depth is not negotiable thanks to the limestone karst subgrade and local frost depth of 36 inches. A typical asphalt driveway should have at least 6–8 inches of compacted, clean stone base; for concrete, a minimum of 8 inches is smart. Older Bethlehem or Easton driveways often were built with just a few inches of gravel, resulting in more failures over time. If a contractor quotes less, especially in older neighborhoods with known subgrade movement issues, you risk early failure and settlement regardless of what’s on top. Skimping here is false economy.
Can I pave over my existing driveway? In some cases, yes—a sound base with only surface cracking can be overlaid, saving money. However, in Lehigh with our freeze-thaw cycles and karst soils, you need a careful inspection first. If you see spiderweb alligator cracks, dips, or repeated potholes, an overlay is just putting off the inevitable and your money is better spent on full replacement (which runs $3,500–$9,000 for asphalt). A reputable contractor in Lehigh will check the base depth and compaction, not just take a quick look and suggest a layover.
Are door-to-door pavers a problem in Lehigh? Yes, they show up in Lehigh and all over Northampton County, usually offering bargain pricing to “use up extra asphalt from another job.” These crews rarely build to current standards and often cut corners on the base—a serious issue here with our difficult soil. Even if the upfront price is tempting ($1,500–$4,500), these jobs almost never last. If they won’t give you a written contract and won’t list a Pennsylvania HIC registration, send them packing. Your risk is much higher here because fixing base or drainage problems left behind can cost more than doing the job right the first time.
How do I verify a paving contractor in Pennsylvania? For any driveway project over $500 in Pennsylvania, the contractor must have a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration. You can verify this for yourself at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. On top of that, ask for proof of workers’ compensation insurance—state law mandates this for any employer. Verification can be done via the WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). Only sign a written contract, never pay the full amount up front, and be aware that you have a three-business-day right of cancellation by state law. Especially in Lehigh, with frequent fly-by-night outfits, these are your best shields against bad outcomes.
How long should an asphalt driveway last in Lehigh? With proper installation—that means a full-depth, well-compacted stone base and attention to drainage—an asphalt driveway in Lehigh’s climate should last 20–30 years. The wide swing comes down to initial construction quality and ongoing maintenance, especially crack filling and keeping water moving. Freeze-thaw stress and limestone karst settlement make shortcuts here especially costly. Older homes with original, thin drives may only get 10–15 years after a basic overlay, but a new, deep-build driveway to today’s standards should last decades if maintained.
Does sealcoating fix cracks? No. Sealcoating does not and cannot fix actual cracks—especially not in Lehigh, where freeze-thaw action will separate even a professionally applied seal from a moving crack in just one winter. Its purpose is to protect asphalt from sun, air, and minor oil/fuel spills, not to address open gaps. Actual crack filling (costing $200–$700 for a standard drive) is the only cost-effective approach for surface cracks under a quarter inch. For cracks wider or with base movement, replacement or full-depth patching is necessary. Don’t let anyone tell you a $400 sealcoat solves more.
How long does a driveway installation take? For a standard Lehigh single-family driveway, a full asphalt replacement is usually completed in 2–4 days from excavation to final roll. Weather, square footage, and the need for regrading or drainage work are the main variables. Concrete drives add a few days because of cure times. If your contractor promises next-day turnaround on significant excavation jobs, be wary—it often means corners cut under the surface. For overlays, one to two days is typical, but again, only if the base is in truly good shape. Rushed projects in Northampton County’s tricky soils are more likely to fail.
