Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors that do work over $500 are generally required to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before you sign anything, verify the contractor’s registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. After storms, door-to-door “storm chasers” sometimes show up with quick offers—if they can’t provide a verifiable PA HIC registration, that’s a major red flag.

Permits are often the confusing part. In many PA municipalities, residential driveway paving itself typically does not require a building permit. However, the driveway approach where your driveway meets the public road may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality—especially if regrading affects the right-of-way, drainage, or the curb/sidewalk area. If any excavation changes runoff patterns, you’re also in the neighborhood of Pennsylvania stormwater expectations (Chapter 102): the paving plan should not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. Contractors should provide a grading approach that controls ponding and manages sheet flow.

You should also verify workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Finally, for Derry homeowners, keep it simple: ask for the contractor’s HIC number, a certificate of insurance (liability), and proof of workers’ compensation before work starts.

  • Confirm HIC registration online (and match the company name exactly).
  • Request a COI for general liability and ensure it’s active.
  • Verify workers’ comp at wcais.pa.gov.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Derry driveway?

For Derry homeowners, the freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor in asphalt vs. concrete. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, which means it typically handles freeze-thaw cracking better, especially when the base is rebuilt and properly compacted. That’s also why maintenance matters: with asphalt, homeowners should plan on sealcoating every 2–3 years to protect the surface and slow water infiltration. Concrete is rigid, and while it can last a long time, it’s more vulnerable to spalling and cracking when road salt and repeated freeze-thaw cycles stress the surface.

In Derry, the housing age matters too. With about 53.9% of the local housing stock built before 1980, many drives are already at or beyond their ideal performance window, and the “right answer” often becomes full replacement rather than resurfacing. If you already know your base is compromised—settling, pumping, or widespread cracking—an overlay usually can’t fix the root cause. In that situation, paying for a full-depth asphalt install (often in the $7,000–$15,000 band) can be a better value than spending in the resurfacing range ($3,500–$9,000) that may only buy a shorter runway.

Concrete can still be the right move when you want a low-maintenance, long-term finish and you can budget for the $9,000–$18,000 range. For example, if you’re replacing a driveway with heavy vehicle traffic and you want fewer routine maintenance steps, concrete’s higher upfront cost may be justified—assuming proper base prep and joints are done correctly. But if your priority is flexibility through Central PA winters and you’re willing to maintain the surface, asphalt is often the practical choice.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in Derry

Choosing the right contractor matters just as much as the material you pick. Start by verifying licensing and certifications. For PA Home Improvement Contractor requirements, check the company’s HIC registration at the Attorney General’s HIC registry. If the contractor has IICRC credentials (often helpful for documentation and quality practices), ask for their certification details and confirm them through the appropriate professional registry.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. You want a breakdown that separates labor and materials (base stone, asphalt mix, removal, haul-off, and drainage/grading work). If a quote is only one lump sum, it’s harder to compare scope and harder to hold the contractor to the plan if something changes. Also read what’s excluded: for example, some bids exclude permits, concrete edging, or drainage correction—those “extras” can move your final total.

Ask about warranties in writing. Good workmanship warranties should be clearly stated (length and what they cover). Confirm whether there’s also an equipment/materials warranty from suppliers. For payment, a safe rule is never paying more than 10–15% upfront and holding the remainder until the job is complete and walk-through items are resolved. Finally, don’t rely on verbal promises: request an arrival estimate and completion window in writing, since lead times in the Harrisburg area can stretch into 4–8 weeks in peak season.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Derry

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

In Derry and across Dauphin County, thickness is one of the best predictors of how long your driveway will hold up through the freeze-thaw cycle. For most durable residential replacements, installers plan for about 3–4 inches of asphalt placed in two lifts over an 8–12 inch compacted stone base. If you’re doing an overlay, the design changes: a resurfacing approach usually assumes the existing base is stable and focuses on adding a thinner asphalt layer (often around a 2-inch overlay), which is why base condition is critical. If the old asphalt is thin (like 1–2 inches) and the stone beneath is shallow or poorly compacted, a thin overlay can fail early due to water infiltration and winter heaving. That’s the difference between jobs in the $7,000–$15,000 replacement range versus those in the $3,500–$9,000 resurfacing range. A good contractor will measure, inspect cracking patterns (including alligator cracking), and recommend the thickness that matches the base and drainage—not just the “standard” on their truck.

How long does driveway paving take in Derry?

For many Derry homeowners, the schedule surprises are less about the paving day and more about lead time and prep. In peak season around the Harrisburg area, it’s common to see lead times stretch into 4–8 weeks, especially when contractors are handling multiple full replacements across townships. On your property, the actual paving work can be fast if the scope is straightforward: a day or two for removal, base grading/compaction, and hauling; then a paving day (or two lifts across the day) plus rolling. Concrete is usually different—pouring and curing means longer waiting before you can fully use the driveway. Asphalt also needs proper cure time after final placement and striping/edge finishing; you’ll typically be asked to limit traffic while it cools and sets.

Weather matters in Central PA. Heavy rain, freezing temperatures, or wet subgrade can push work back to protect your base and compaction. Your contractor should give you a written arrival estimate and completion window before the crew mobilizes.

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in Derry?

Surface cracking alone doesn’t always mean the base is failing, but in Derry’s climate, certain symptoms are strong indicators that water is getting in and the stone/base isn’t holding. Look for areas where asphalt is “alligator” cracking with interconnected breaks, sections that have settled or “dip” compared to the rest of the driveway, or edges that crumble and pull away. If you’ve noticed ponding after rain—especially in low spots—those trapped-water areas are prime candidates for freeze-thaw heaving, which pushes against the asphalt and eventually opens the crack network. Another sign is repeated patching that only lasts a short time; if a contractor keeps fixing the top but the problem returns quickly, the base may be unstable.

In practice, base failure is why Derry homeowners often move from resurfacing into full replacement. Resurfacing in the $3,500–$9,000 band can be a good value when the base is sound. But when the base is the problem, a full replacement in the $7,000–$15,000 range tends to be the durable fix because the excavation and rebuilt compacted stone base are what stop water-driven deterioration.

How much does driveway paving cost in Derry, PA?

Driveway paving costs in Derry depend on how much you need to remove, how much base you have to rebuild, and how thick the new pavement needs to be for Pennsylvania’s winter cycle. For asphalt driveway replacement with new base, typical estimates in Dauphin County land in the $7,000–$15,000 range. If the existing base is structurally sound and you’re mainly treating surface cracking, resurfacing/overlay is often in the $3,500–$9,000 band. Sealcoating is much lower cost and works as maintenance on sound asphalt, usually around $300–$900 per application, but it won’t correct a failing base.

Concrete driveways usually cost more upfront, with new installs often in the $9,000–$18,000 range depending on thickness, reinforcement, and site prep. Your driveway size and drainage conditions are usually the biggest cost drivers, because they determine excavation depth, stone tonnage, and asphalt quantity.

How long does a new asphalt driveway last in Derry's climate?

A properly built new asphalt driveway in Derry—meaning adequate compacted stone base, correct asphalt thickness in two lifts, and proper drainage/grade—commonly lasts 20–30 years. The climate contribution is real: Central PA’s freeze-thaw cycle plus salt use can accelerate aging if the surface is left unprotected. That’s why sealcoating is a practical part of the ownership plan. Preventive sealcoating every 2–3 years (often around $300–$900 per application for a standard residential driveway) helps reduce water infiltration and protects the binder from drying.

Maintenance timing matters. A driveway that’s built right but neglected on sealing can shorten its useful life, while one that’s sealed consistently tends to stay serviceable longer even when winters are rough. If you see recurring wet spots, drainage issues, or deep cracking returning after patching, those are usually base or grading problems—addressing them is what preserves the expected lifespan.

Should I repair or replace my driveway in Derry?

“Repair” makes sense when the pavement is mostly stable and damage is limited to minor cracks that haven’t penetrated into a failed base. In Derry, crack filling and patching only are typically for minor cracking (often under about 1/4 inch) and can be a short-term fix—around $200–$800 per application—with a lifespan roughly 1–3 years depending on water management and traffic. If the driveway is structurally failing—settlement, extensive cracking patterns, or areas that heave or pond—replacement is usually the smarter decision. That’s especially common in older homes and driveways, and roughly 53.9% of local housing was built before 1980, which frequently correlates with shallow base and older drainage practices.

Resurfacing is the middle path. If the base is sound but the surface is tired, overlay/resurfacing in the $3,500–$9,000 range can add years of service (commonly 8–15 years). The best answer comes from a contractor who checks the base condition and drainage, not just the top layer. If you’re unsure, ask for a base assessment during the estimate.
