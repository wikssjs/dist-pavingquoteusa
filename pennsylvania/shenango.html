Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors typically must be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office if the work exceeds $500. Before you sign anything, verify the contractor’s status at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be extra cautious if anyone is soliciting driveway work door-to-door after a storm (“storm chasers”) and cannot provide a verifiable PA HIC registration.

Permits: residential driveway paving often does not require a building permit in many PA municipalities, but the driveway approach—where your driveway meets the public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. Requirements can change when you alter the driveway footprint, raise/lower grade, or tie into sidewalks. We recommend confirming the permit path before excavation, especially in Shenango areas near curbs, sidewalks, or township-maintained roadways.

Water runoff: Pennsylvania stormwater rules (Chapter 102) require that paving projects do not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public right-of-way. That means proper grading and, when needed, drainage solutions (like swales or directed flow) should be part of the scope.

Workers’ compensation matters, too. Verify coverage at wcais.pa.gov. An uninsured crew can expose homeowners to liability.

How to verify step-by-step: (1) check HIC online; (2) request a current certificate of insurance showing general liability and auto (if equipment is used on-site); (3) ask for proof of workers’ compensation coverage; (4) confirm whether a driveway access permit is required based on your approach to the road. If the contractor can’t provide these items promptly, that’s a major red flag.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Shenango driveway?

In Shenango, PA, the freeze–thaw cycle is the deciding factor for many homeowners choosing asphalt versus concrete. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently under repeated moisture and road-salt exposure. Asphalt tends to stay flexible, so it handles freeze–thaw stress better with the right maintenance—especially sealcoating. Concrete is rigid and can spall or crack when salts and moisture get into small surface defects, which is why good base support, proper thickness, and weather-smart finishing matter.

Local housing age affects expectations. With 62.1% of homes built before 1980 (ACS 2024), many driveways in Lawrence County are already at the stage where the original base may be compromised. If the base has begun to move, resurfacing or “thin repair” won’t hold. That’s where full asphalt replacement (typically within the $4,500–$13,000 range) can be justified because it rebuilds the stone base and adds an asphalt thickness designed to last.

Concrete may still be the better choice for some properties—especially where you want a longer maintenance-free window and can manage cost. A real decision point: if your asphalt replacement quote lands around $6,500 and your concrete bid comes in around $11,000, the asphalt difference is large enough that many Shenango homeowners choose asphalt now and plan preventive maintenance. However, if you’re replacing a driveway where heavy vehicles and years of freeze–thaw have already created ongoing patch cycles, concrete’s longer lifespan (30–50 years) can be worth the premium when installed over a properly prepared base.

For most older Shenango drives, asphalt remains the most “forgiving” option through winter—but only when sealcoating is scheduled appropriately and the base is rebuilt when it’s failed.

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in Shenango?

In Shenango and across Lawrence County, failing base usually shows up as more than just surface cracking. Look for rutting that worsens after spring thaw, depressions that “trap” water near the garage or along one edge, and alligator cracking that expands over time even after patching. Edge breakup—where the asphalt crumbles first near the driveway border—is another base movement clue, especially on older pre-1980 drives. If you’ve seen repeated potholes returning in the same spots, that often means the stone base underneath has lost stability or drainage is directing water to the same area. Weather matters here: Pennsylvania’s freeze–thaw cycle turns trapped moisture into expansion and heaving, accelerating failure. When base is compromised, sealcoating usually won’t solve the problem; instead, the project typically needs resurfacing if the base is sound, or full replacement if the cracking and depressions indicate movement. That’s why homeowner quotes can vary: fixing the surface only versus rebuilding base can shift you from the $2,500–$8,000 overlay band into the $4,500–$13,000 full replacement range.

With proper base preparation and correct asphalt thickness, a new asphalt driveway in Shenango typically lasts about 20–30 years. The Pennsylvania freeze–thaw cycle is the reason good construction details matter: adequate stone base, proper grading so water doesn’t pond, and enough asphalt thickness help the driveway resist rutting and cracking. When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Shenango?

For most Shenango asphalt driveways, sealcoating is a preventive maintenance step—not a fix for failed base. A common schedule is every 2–3 years, which helps protect against oxidation, surface raveling, and the impact of winter conditions. In practical terms, you typically wait until the asphalt has cured and then seal when the surface is still intact: light surface wear, minor cracks, and no signs of base movement are ideal. If you have widespread alligator cracking, depressions, or edge breakup that indicates underlying instability, sealcoating will not stop the movement—resurfacing or full replacement is usually the correct scope. Timing also matters: avoid sealing late in the season when temperatures and curing conditions are poor. If you want to budget, sealcoating commonly runs about $300–$900 per standard driveway application depending on size and prep needs. If your driveway has been patched repeatedly, ask for an assessment before sealing; many homeowners in older Lawrence County neighborhoods (with a high share of pre-1980 homes, ACS 2024) spend less long-term when they seal only structurally sound asphalt.
