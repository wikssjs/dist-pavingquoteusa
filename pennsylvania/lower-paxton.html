For example, if your driveway needs full replacement, the typical band in this market runs about $7,000–$15,000; if the base is truly sound and only the surface is failing, resurfacing is often closer to $3,500–$9,000. Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors who do work over $500 are typically required to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before you sign anything, verify the contractor at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Also watch for “storm chaser” activity after wind, flooding, or heavy rain—door-to-door solicitation without a verifiable PA HIC registration is a major red flag.

Permits: residential driveway paving usually does not require a building permit in most PA municipalities, but the driveway approach—where it meets the public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. Regrading, significant curb/sidewalk tie-ins, or changes near the right-of-way are the situations most likely to trigger permit review. If your plan affects stormwater flow, you should assume review is needed before excavation.

Water runoff: PA stormwater requirements under Chapter 102 generally require that paving projects don’t increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. A reputable contractor will plan grading and, when needed, add drainage solutions so meltwater and stormwater don’t pool and then freeze.

Workers’ compensation: confirm coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can create liability risk for property owners.

Step-by-step for Lower Paxton homeowners: (1) check the PA HIC record online, (2) request a certificate of insurance showing general liability (and auto if applicable), (3) verify workers’ compensation coverage through the WC registry, and (4) confirm with your contractor whether a driveway access/approach permit is required based on where the work touches the roadway.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Lower Paxton driveway?

For Lower Paxton homeowners, the Pennsylvania freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor in the asphalt-versus-concrete conversation. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, so it typically handles freeze-thaw movement well when it’s installed on a properly compacted stone base. In practice, asphalt also performs best when maintained—sealcoating every 2–3 years helps slow oxidation and surface raveling so water doesn’t work its way deeper.

Concrete is rigid. It can be an excellent long-term option, but freeze-thaw cycling plus road salt exposure can contribute to surface deterioration and cracking patterns if the base isn’t ideal or if deicers are used incorrectly. In Dauphin County, where winters bring multiple freeze-thaw events, concrete driveways can still last 30–50 years, yet repair needs may show up earlier if water infiltrates under the slab.

Grounding this in local housing stock, about 52.2% of Lower Paxton homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024). That age often means existing driveways are at or beyond their expected service life—so full replacement is more common than “quick resurfacing.”

Here’s a concrete cost example: if your driveway needs full depth repair, moving from resurfacing (roughly $3,500–$9,000) to full replacement (roughly $7,000–$15,000) may be justified. The same logic applies to material choice—if your base is being rebuilt, either option can be durable. If your base is unsound, overlaying asphalt or placing concrete over problematic soils can reduce longevity, and you’ll pay again sooner.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Lower Paxton

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Lower Paxton?

In Lower Paxton, sealcoating works best as preventive maintenance—typically every 2–3 years—when the asphalt is still structurally sound. If your driveway is showing minor surface wear and small cracks (not deep alligator cracking or settled areas), sealcoating can help slow oxidation and reduce water penetration from freeze-thaw cycles. As a reference point for planning, standard sealcoating is often in the $300–$900 range for a typical driveway, depending on size and surface prep. If you’re seeing ponding at the garage approach or the pavement has soft spots, sealcoating alone won’t fix the underlying base/support issue. In that case, a contractor should investigate base condition before recommending resurfacing or full replacement. Also ask whether crack filling is being done before sealcoat, because sealing over untreated active cracks usually shortens performance in our PA winter conditions.

What causes driveway cracks in Lower Paxton?

Driveway cracking in Lower Paxton is usually a combination of water and movement. Older Dauphin County driveways often have thinner asphalt and shallower stone base, so water can migrate under the pavement and freeze during cold snaps. When water freezes, it expands, pushing pavement upward and creating wider cracks over time (freeze-thaw cycle damage). Cracks can also start as reflective cracks from areas of settlement, then develop into alligator cracking if the base can’t support the load. Another common cause is poor drainage or insufficient slope—if the driveway holds water near the garage, you’ll see faster deterioration along the low spots. If your cracks are under about 1/4 inch and your driveway is relatively level, crack filling and patching may help for 1–3 years, typically $200–$800. If the driveway is uneven or the cracks are interconnected, it’s often a base-prep problem that requires overlay or full replacement rather than repeated patchwork.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in Lower Paxton?

Sometimes, but it depends on what caused the damage and what your policy covers. Many homeowner policies handle sudden, accidental events—like a tree fall, vehicle impact, or a weather event covered under “loss” definitions—rather than normal wear from freeze-thaw cycles. In Lower Paxton and across Pennsylvania, routine pavement deterioration (cracking, settlement, and surface raveling) usually isn’t covered as a maintenance issue. If you think you qualify under an accidental claim, document everything: photos from multiple angles, measurements of settlement/ponding, and repair estimates from a contractor. Ask the contractor for written documentation describing the observed damage, whether the issue appears structural (base failure) or surface-level, and what repair option is recommended. For structural failures, insurers may require additional support evidence. Also note that if runoff and drainage are involved, the project may need compliance with stormwater expectations (Chapter 102) so repairs don’t increase runoff to neighboring properties or the right-of-way.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

There isn’t a single “one number” for every Lower Paxton driveway, because thickness depends on the condition of the existing base (or what’s being rebuilt), how much excavation is required, and the intended use. That said, durable Central PA residential installs often involve rebuilding to an 8–12 inch compacted stone base and installing about 3–4 inches of asphalt in two lifts for full-depth projects, which is designed to better resist our freeze-thaw cycle and salt exposure. If the base is sound and only the surface is cracking, resurfacing/overlay is sometimes appropriate and is usually in the $3,500–$9,000 band—typically with a thinner asphalt layer than full replacement. If your driveway has failed base, settled sections, or extensive cracking (including alligator cracking), a “thin overlay” approach often shortens lifespan because the pavement flexes over an unsupportive foundation. A good contractor will core or inspect representative areas and explain what thickness and base depth are being built—specifically for your site.

How long does driveway paving take in Lower Paxton?

For many Lower Paxton residential projects, the actual paving day(s) are shorter than people expect, but prep and scheduling drive the timeline. Typical driveway paving work can take a few days once crews mobilize: demolition/excavation and base preparation first, then paving in lifts, then final finishing and curing/setup. Asphalt placement can be fast, but base compaction and drainage/grading must be done correctly to avoid early failure. Your schedule can also be affected by lead times in the Harrisburg-area market—especially during peak season, when lead times can stretch into the 4–8 week range. Weather matters too because asphalt installation depends on temperatures and surface moisture. Ask the contractor for a written arrival estimate and completion window before signing, and clarify whether crack sealing, sealcoating, or striping is included or separate (sealcoating timing depends on asphalt curing). If permits or driveway approach review is required for changes near the public road, that can add additional calendar time.

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in Lower Paxton?

Failing base usually shows up as symptoms that go beyond “surface cracks.” Look for interconnected alligator cracking, repeated patch failures, and areas that appear to settle or dip—especially near the garage apron or where vehicles turn. Ponding water after rain is another big clue: if the driveway doesn’t drain properly and stays wet, freeze-thaw cycles can push up pavement and widen cracks. Edge failures can also indicate base movement, such as asphalt breaking away from curbing or crumbling near sidewalks. If you’ve had crack filling or patching done before and the same zones return quickly, that suggests water is getting under the pavement and the foundation isn’t stable. In those situations, maintenance items alone (like sealcoating) won’t correct the underlying issue. The repair recommendation often shifts toward overlay or full replacement, which in this market can move from resurfacing at about $3,500–$9,000 to full replacement around $7,000–$15,000, depending on base removal and rebuild scope.
