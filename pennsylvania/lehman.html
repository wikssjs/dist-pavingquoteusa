Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors who perform home improvement work that exceeds $500 generally must be registered as a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before you sign anything, verify the contractor’s HIC registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Also be cautious of “storm chaser” crews—especially after heavy weather—who solicit driveway work door-to-door without clear, verifiable PA HIC registration.

Permits: residential driveway paving often does not require a building permit in many PA municipalities, but the driveway approach—where your driveway meets a public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. Before excavation, confirm whether your site needs approval, particularly if the plan changes frontage, alters the curb line, or involves widening near the right-of-way.

Runoff: Pennsylvania’s stormwater requirements (Chapter 102) require that paving projects do not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. Proper grading, swales, and in some cases drainage structures may be needed—especially if your driveway slopes toward a neighbor or toward a low spot.

Workers’ compensation: confirm coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews put you at risk.

Step-by-step for Lehman homeowners: (1) check HIC registration online; (2) request a current certificate of insurance for general liability; (3) ask for proof of workers’ compensation coverage and confirm via the WC system; (4) ensure your written proposal includes the scope for grading and drainage; and (5) confirm whether a driveway access permit is required before work begins.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Lehman driveway?

Pennsylvania’s freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor for many Lehman homeowners. Asphalt and concrete both move with temperature changes, but they fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, so it generally handles freeze-thaw without the same rigid cracking pattern—when the base is properly prepared. Asphalt also benefits from routine maintenance: sealcoating is typically done every 2–3 years to slow oxidation and keep water from penetrating the surface. Concrete is rigid and can be durable, but in Pike County it can spall or crack when moisture and salt exposure combine with multiple winter freeze-thaw cycles; joints and drainage still matter.

Lehman’s housing age affects the decision too. With 19.1% of homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many driveways are at or beyond the point where the base has settled or the edges have broken down. When the underlying base is failing, resurfacing options won’t fix the root cause—full replacement is usually the safer bet. That’s why an overlay may make sense only when the base is solid and you’re seeing surface cracking without rutting. If you’re dealing with failed base and recurring heaving, the “more now” cost of full replacement in the $6,500–$14,500 band can be justified compared to trying to maintain an asphalt driveway that keeps coming back.

Here’s a simple rule of thumb: choose asphalt when you want flexibility and you’re willing to maintain with sealcoating; choose concrete when you want long life and low ongoing coating needs, and you can ensure proper base and jointing. In some cases, homeowners prefer concrete for the first 10–20 feet to reduce wear at the garage approach while keeping asphalt elsewhere—if drainage and elevations are handled correctly.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Lehman

Should I repair or replace my driveway in Lehman?

In Lehman, the decision usually comes down to what’s happening under the surface. If you’re seeing minor cracks and a few small patches, crack filling and patching can be an appropriate short-term move—especially when the cracks are under about 1/4 inch and the base feels stable. But if you have rutting, repeating settlement, failed edges, or alligator cracking that’s spreading, repairs often become a cycle. In those cases, full replacement is typically the better long-term value; for many Lehman homeowners, full replacement projects commonly fall in the $6,500–$14,500 range depending on size and how much base must be rebuilt.

Pike County’s freeze-thaw cycle amplifies base problems. When water gets into weak layers, winter heaving and spring settlement can make thin or patched sections fail early. If the driveway is structurally sound but only the top is aging, resurfacing/overlay in the $3,000–$8,000 band may extend life. The key is having a contractor evaluate base conditions (not just the asphalt surface) and recommend the option that matches the actual cause.

How do I find a reputable paving contractor in Lehman, PA?

Start with credential verification and then move to scope clarity. For driveway work in Pennsylvania above $500, confirm the contractor’s Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration through the PA Attorney General’s office at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Next, verify workers’ compensation at wcais.pa.gov and request a current certificate of insurance for general liability. A reputable contractor should provide these without pressure.

Then ask for 2–3 written, itemized estimates. You should see details like base excavation depth, compacted base thickness, asphalt or concrete thickness, tack coat, disposal/hauling if removal is included, and any drainage grading. If a quote is only a lump sum, it’s harder to compare apples-to-apples and easier for “extras” to appear later.

Finally, request warranty terms for workmanship and materials, confirm the payment schedule (never more than about 10–15% upfront), and get a written arrival estimate and completion window. In Lehman, weather and capacity can change lead times, so clarity upfront reduces surprises.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Lehman?

Sealcoating is a preventive maintenance step, not a structural fix. In Lehman’s Pike County climate, sealcoating is typically scheduled about every 2–3 years when the asphalt is still structurally sound and the surface is clean and dry. If your driveway has visible failures like potholes, significant rutting, or base-related cracking that’s opening and moving, sealcoating will not solve the underlying issue—those problems require patching, overlay, or full replacement.

When the asphalt is in good condition, sealcoating generally supports pavement longevity. For homeowners planning ahead, sealcoating costs commonly land in the $250–$900 range depending on driveway size, surface prep, and how many coats are recommended. Timing matters: applying too late after oxidation has advanced can limit how much benefit you get, while applying too soon before the asphalt has cured can trap moisture.

For best results, a contractor should assess whether crack filling is needed first and whether drainage is adequate so water isn’t pooling on the surface during melt cycles.

What causes driveway cracks in Lehman?

Driveway cracking in Lehman is usually a mix of normal aging and site-specific stress. The biggest drivers we see in Pike County include water intrusion into weak base layers, freeze-thaw expansion, and edge failure. When water penetrates the asphalt, winter heaving can lift material slightly; then spring settlement pulls it back. Over time, that movement can turn fine shrinkage cracks into wider cracks or even alligator cracking.

Cracks can also show up sooner if the asphalt thickness is too thin for local conditions or if the base was compacted inconsistently. Home driveways that pond near a garage or where downspouts discharge toward the pavement are especially prone to repeating damage—because ponded water has more time to infiltrate during temperature swings.

If the cracks are minor and the base is still stable, crack filling is often appropriate as a maintenance step—often in the $200–$800 range depending on how much needs to be addressed. But if cracking is widespread and accompanied by softness or failed edges, maintenance alone won’t stop recurrence, and an overlay (often $3,000–$8,000) or full replacement (often $6,500–$14,500) may be the practical solution.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in Lehman?

Sometimes, but it depends heavily on the cause and the specific policy. Most homeowner’s insurance is designed to cover sudden, accidental events—like a vehicle impact, a falling tree, or specific storm-related damage—not typical wear from freeze-thaw cycling, aging, or general pavement deterioration. In Lehman and throughout Pennsylvania, freeze-thaw damage and normal asphalt oxidation typically aren’t treated as a covered loss unless it follows a clearly documented, sudden incident.

To check coverage, review your declarations page and policy section for “perils covered,” and ask your agent what applies to “driveways,” “other structures,” or “landscaping.” It’s also helpful to document the condition with photos and, if needed, written notes from a contractor explaining what happened (for example, impact versus base failure). If your claim hinges on workmanship or design issues, you’ll want detailed documentation and a clear scope.

Whether or not insurance pays, it’s important to choose the right fix. Sealcoating and crack filling are protective and helpful for structurally sound asphalt, but they won’t correct base failure that keeps coming back after winters.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

There isn’t a single magic thickness for every Lehman driveway, because thickness depends on the current base condition and whether you’re doing an overlay or a full-depth installation. In general, full replacements are designed with enough asphalt depth to handle Pennsylvania freeze-thaw stresses, heavy seasonal moisture, and driveway loads. Overlays can use less asphalt height, but only when the existing base is stable and properly prepared—otherwise the overlay just traps problems and fails sooner.

Many homeowners compare options using the typical price bands. For instance, resurfacing/overlay often lands in the $3,000–$8,000 range when the base can stay in place and the project scope is mostly surface corrections. Full replacement projects commonly fall in the $6,500–$14,500 range when base rebuilding is required. Those price differences usually reflect whether a deeper asphalt structure is included and whether excavation/compaction is needed.

The right way to confirm thickness for your driveway is to have a contractor remove failed areas or evaluate base conditions onsite and specify the planned asphalt thickness and compaction method in writing. That’s what prevents early failure in Pike County winters.
