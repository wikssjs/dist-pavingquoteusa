Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

For work in Pennsylvania, a key rule is contractor eligibility: if the paving project exceeds $500, the paving contractor generally must be registered as a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. You can verify this at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. After storms, you may see “storm chasers.” If someone is door-to-door and can’t provide verifiable HIC registration, that’s a significant red flag.

Permits are more situational. Residential driveway paving usually does not require a building permit in most municipalities, but driveway approaches—where the driveway meets a public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. If any part of the job touches the roadway apron/approach, check before excavation.

Runoff matters in PA: under PA stormwater requirements (commonly referenced through Chapter 102), paving projects should not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. A reputable contractor will address grading and may propose drainage corrections rather than simply adding asphalt.

Also verify insurance and liability exposure. Confirm workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews can create risk for you as the property owner.

How homeowners in Elizabethtown should verify credentials, step-by-step: (1) check the contractor’s HIC status online, (2) request a current Certificate of Insurance for general liability and auto (if applicable), (3) provide proof of workers’ compensation coverage and verify it at wcais.pa.gov, (4) confirm the scope includes any required driveway approach permits, and (5) ask for documentation in writing before scheduling.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Elizabethtown driveway?

In Elizabethtown, Pennsylvania, the freeze–thaw cycle is the defining factor when deciding between asphalt and concrete. Asphalt and concrete both expand and contract with temperature swings, but they tend to fail differently. Asphalt is more flexible, so it’s generally more resistant to freeze–thaw cracking. That flexibility comes with a maintenance reality: properly sealed asphalt typically needs sealcoating every 2–3 years to slow oxidation and protect the surface from salt and moisture intrusion. Concrete, on the other hand, is rigid. In Lancaster County, winters often bring multiple freeze–thaw cycles per season and de-icing exposure, which can contribute to spalling and cracking that may require repair before it becomes a larger problem.

Because 67.9% of homes were built before 1980 (ACS 2024), many existing driveways are already beyond the point where resurfacing is the best answer. In those cases, replacement can be the most cost-effective path. For example, a driveway that’s heavily cracked and settled may cost less upfront to overlay (roughly $2,500–$6,000), but if the base is compromised, that overlay can fail prematurely and force another teardown. Full replacement (often $4,500–$9,000 for asphalt) costs more, yet it addresses the underlying issue—so it typically performs better over the next several winters.

So if your driveway is in good shape structurally, asphalt resurfacing or sealcoating may fit. If you’re dealing with failed base, widespread fatigue cracking, and drainage problems, full replacement—whether asphalt or concrete—usually delivers the best long-term outcome for Elizabethtown homeowners.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in Elizabethtown

Choosing the right paving contractor in Elizabethtown isn’t just about the lowest number. Start by verifying contractor credentials. In Pennsylvania, many driveway projects require the contractor to be registered as a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC). Check HIC status at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. For workers’ compensation coverage, verify at wcais.pa.gov. If the contractor mentions industry certification (often IICRC), ask for the specific certificate number and verify it through the certifying body’s records when available.

Next, request 2–3 written, itemized quotes. A solid quote breaks down labor and materials (excavation, base, asphalt or concrete, edging/apron work, and any patching/crack filling). Avoid offers that only provide a lump sum without explaining what’s included and what isn’t—especially around drainage correction and base preparation depth, where most “surprises” occur.

Read the scope carefully for exclusions: soil hauling, disposal, sawcutting, permit handling, drainage adjustments, and whether traffic control is included. Ask whether the quote includes documentation if insurance claim paperwork is involved. Warranty matters too: confirm the workmanship warranty length and whether there’s also a materials warranty, including who supplies the asphalt/concrete and how product issues are handled.

Payment schedule is another big indicator. Never pay more than 10–15% upfront. Hold back a portion until the job is complete and clean-up is verified. Finally, ask for a written arrival estimate and a completion window (Lancaster County peak season often brings 4–8 week lead times), so the schedule is realistic.

How long does a new asphalt driveway last in Elizabethtown's climate?

Lancaster County’s climate—multiple freeze–thaw cycles and de-icing—can shorten service life if water is allowed to pond or if the base isn’t rebuilt where it has weakened. Should I repair or replace my driveway in Elizabethtown?

Repair vs. replace depends on what’s driving the damage. In Elizabethtown, small cracks that are narrow (often under 1/4 inch) and limited to the surface may be handled with crack filling and patching, typically a short-term solution in the $200–$800 band per application. However, when you see widespread cracking, interconnected “alligator” patterns, or areas that have settled or hold water, repair alone usually can’t fix the underlying issue. In that situation, an overlay may not be appropriate because water intrusion and freeze–thaw heaving continue from the base upward. Full replacement is usually the better long-term value, especially with older driveways common in Pennsylvania’s 67.9% pre-1980 housing profile. If you’re being offered only cosmetic patching while the base looks failed, ask for a clear explanation of why an overlay or tear-out isn’t recommended. When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Elizabethtown?

Sealcoating timing depends on the asphalt age and condition. For most new asphalt, you typically wait until it’s properly cured and free of surface issues before sealcoating (your contractor can confirm the readiness based on the mix and weather window). For driveways that are already established and still structurally sound, plan on sealcoating every 2–3 years in the Elizabethtown area. That schedule aligns with how Lancaster County’s freeze–thaw cycle and de-icing exposure accelerate oxidation and moisture damage. What causes driveway cracks in Elizabethtown?

Driveway cracks in Elizabethtown usually come from a mix of water, temperature movement, and base performance. The biggest contributor in Lancaster County is freeze–thaw: when water penetrates through the surface and reaches the base, it expands during freezing and contracts when thawing, stressing the pavement. Over time, this leads to fatigue cracking and, if water is recurring, potholes and heaving in low spots. Poor drainage or inadequate slope can make the problem worse because water can pond rather than run off toward a proper discharge area. Another cause is base settlement—common on older driveways in Pennsylvania’s pre-1980 housing stock—where compaction and stone depth weren’t designed for today’s traffic patterns. Finally, repeated de-icing and winter sanding can hasten surface breakdown, especially when oxidation has already started. If the cracks are minor and narrow, crack filling may be appropriate; if cracking is widespread, you’ll want a structural assessment before choosing an overlay or full replacement.
