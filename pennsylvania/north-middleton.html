Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, your contractor’s credentials and the right approvals matter. If the paving work exceeds $500, Pennsylvania requires Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) registration through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before you sign anything, verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be especially cautious about “storm chaser” solicitation—door-to-door offers after storms without verifiable PA HIC registration are a major red flag.

Permits: In many PA municipalities, residential driveway paving itself typically does not require a building permit. However, the driveway approach—where the driveway meets the public road—often needs a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality. This is commonly tied to the curb/sidewalk tie-in and how runoff is handled near the right-of-way. Always check before excavation.

Water runoff: Pennsylvania stormwater rules (Chapter 102) require that paving projects not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. A qualified contractor will plan grading so water sheds correctly and may include drainage solutions where needed.

Workers’ compensation: confirm coverage at wcais.pa.gov. If a contractor’s crew is uninsured, you can be exposed to liability if there’s an injury on your property.

Step-by-step for North Middleton: (1) check HIC status online at the AG site, (2) request a certificate of insurance showing general liability and auto (if applicable), (3) verify workers’ compensation at wcais.pa.gov, (4) ask which permits (driveway access) apply to your approach area, and (5) confirm the plan to manage runoff per local stormwater expectations.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your North Middleton driveway?

For North Middleton homeowners, Pennsylvania’s freeze-thaw cycle is the deciding factor in asphalt versus concrete performance. Asphalt and concrete both expand and contract through temperature swings, but they fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, so it typically handles freeze-thaw stress better and usually resists wide cracking when installed over a properly compacted base. The tradeoff is maintenance—sealcoating is often the difference between an “aged but solid” driveway and one that deteriorates quickly. Asphalt is commonly refreshed on a 2–3 year cycle for preventive protection when the structure is sound.

Concrete is rigid and long-lasting, but it can spall or crack when exposed to road salt, heavy vehicle loads, and repeated freeze-thaw cycling—conditions that are familiar in Cumberland County. In North Middleton, a large share of homes are older (about 53.5% were built before 1980), which means many driveways are at or beyond the point where the underlying base has already been compromised. That’s why we often see full replacement more frequently than simple resurfacing when major grade issues, heaving, or alligator cracking show up.

Here’s a practical way to think about value. If your existing asphalt is structurally sound and you’re mainly dealing with surface wear, sealcoating is an economical maintenance step in the $300–$1,200 range per application. But if the base is failing, paying for overlay can be false economy—full replacement in the $4,500–$12,000 band typically costs more upfront, yet it addresses the root cause rather than coating symptoms.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in North Middleton?

Sealcoating works best as preventive maintenance when the asphalt structure is still sound—think minor surface cracking, not failing edges, ponding, or alligator cracking that suggests base problems. In North Middleton and across Cumberland County, the typical timing is every 2–3 years for routine protection, especially after you’ve winter-tested the driveway with plowing and de-icing. If your driveway is relatively new asphalt, wait until it has cured and the surface is ready to accept the coating; your contractor will assess cleanliness and surface condition before applying. If you notice the pavement is heaving, settling, or cracking widely, sealcoating can hide symptoms but won’t fix the underlying base issue. In those cases, resurfacing or full replacement may be more cost-effective than repeated coats. As a budget reference, sealcoating commonly falls in the $300–$1,200 band per standard residential driveway (depending on size and prep). A good contractor will also recommend crack filling as part of a maintenance plan and will focus on drainage grading so water doesn’t sit under the sealcoated surface.

What causes driveway cracks in North Middleton?

Driveway cracking in North Middleton is usually a combination of base movement and Pennsylvania freeze-thaw cycling. Water is the culprit most homeowners never see: when drainage is poor or slope is off, water infiltrates through cracks, freezes, expands, and pushes the asphalt upward—leading to heaving and eventually widespread cracking. In older neighborhoods where driveways were installed pre-1980 (about 53.5% of homes built before 1980, U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), you’ll often find undersized or improperly compacted stone base. Over decades, that base shifts and stresses the asphalt surface. Common crack types include hairline cracking from aging and oxidation, longitudinal cracks from settlement, and alligator cracking from repeated stress and failing base support. If cracks are small (for example, under about 1/4 inch) and the pavement around them is stable, crack filling and patching may extend life briefly in the $200–$800 range. If cracks are paired with soft spots or significant edge deterioration, sealcoating alone usually isn’t enough—resurfacing or full replacement is often the better solution for long-term performance.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

Asphalt thickness depends on whether you’re doing an overlay or a full-depth rebuild and what your existing base can support. For overlays, contractors often aim for a new asphalt layer thickness that restores durability without exceeding tie-in heights. For full replacement, residential driveways in Pennsylvania commonly use a thicker hot-mix build-up to handle freeze-thaw cycles and heavier loads near garage approaches. The key is not just the asphalt inches—it’s the complete system: proper compacted stone base depth, correct grading, and adequate asphalt thickness in two lifts so compaction is achieved. In North Middleton, older driveways frequently show base distress, and that’s where thickness alone won’t save the pavement if the base is failing. As a price planning reference, full replacement for asphalt typically lands in the $4,500–$12,000 range, while resurfacing/overlay is commonly $2,500–$7,500 when the base is structurally sound.
