Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

What causes driveway cracks in New Kensington?

Driveway cracks in New Kensington are usually the result of several forces acting together: freeze–thaw expansion, water intrusion, and (very commonly) an inadequate or contaminated base. In older Westmoreland County neighborhoods, many driveways were originally built with limited base depth and only a thin asphalt layer; once the top wears, water can migrate down and get trapped. In winter, that trapped moisture expands, contributing to heaving and cracking. Salt use accelerates surface aging and can worsen spalling where water reaches the surface edges. Poor drainage and incorrect slope create ponding, which increases the chances of alligator cracking and pothole formation. Cracks can also form from settlement—especially on driveways tied near aprons or where the ground is clay-leaning. If cracks are thin and stable, crack filling can help. If the cracks are widening, connected, or accompanied by raveling and potholes, that’s often a sign the base needs more than surface patching.
