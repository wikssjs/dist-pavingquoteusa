Getting thirty years out of a driveway

The easiest way to make your driveway last longer in Upper Allen isn’t top-dollar paving—it’s basic maintenance, done on time. Without question, crack filling comes first. Unfilled cracks let water reach the stone base under your asphalt or concrete. Every winter, water freezes, expands, and then thaws, prying those cracks wider and steadily breaking down the base. Even a single season of neglect can start damage that shortens the driveway’s life by years. Next up: edge support. If the sides of your driveway aren't buttressed—by firm soil or stone—the edges break down first. Never drive right to the very edge, especially after a wet spell. For driveways near sloped ground, keep water draining off and away. Proper gutter cleaning and downspout placement can mean the difference between a crack-free driveway and one riddled with potholes after a hard winter. Avoid concentrated loads from dumpsters, campers, or jacks. These spot loads can leave depressions, especially in the early weeks after new paving. For new asphalt, you need to wait two to three days before parking, and a month before risking parked trailers or turning your steering wheel while stationary. In Cumberland County's climate, de-icing deserves thought. Rock salt is a problem for concrete—it causes pitting and flaking over time. On asphalt, it isn’t as chemically destructive, but the bigger issue is the freeze-thaw cycles from melting and re-freezing. Calcium magnesium acetate is a salt alternative but costs more per bag. Sand provides traction without harming the surface. None of these steps are hard or costly individually, but ignoring any can shave five or ten years off a driveway in Upper Allen’s freeze-thaw cycle.

Sealcoating: what it does and what it doesn't

Sealcoating comes up in nearly every conversation about asphalt driveways in Upper Allen, mostly because it’s an easy add-on sale for paving companies. Here’s the truth. Sealcoating does a few things well. First, it shields your driveway’s asphalt binder from sunlight and oxidation, which can make the surface brittle over time. It also blocks fuel and oil leaks from soaking in, and it revives faded, patchy gray into a uniform black—though that’s cosmetic. What sealcoating never does: it cannot mend cracks, rebuild lost thickness, or provide any real structural strength. If your base or the asphalt itself has failed, no sealant will turn back the clock. Where most people go wrong is overdoing it. Applying sealcoat annually is not only unnecessary—it can cause layers to peel and flake, especially in Cumberland County’s climate with its hard freezes. The first coat should wait until a new asphalt surface has cured—usually six to twelve months. After that, every three or four years is enough on a typical Upper Allen driveway. Early sealing traps vapors under the coating, which actually weakens the top layer. Crack filling is more crucial than sealcoating for extending life. Filling is about stopping water; sealing is about surface preservation. A good sealcoating job means thorough cleaning, precision application (not just a quick spray), and no dilution with too much water. Spray-and-go outfits pitching a cheap sealcoat—often at prices well below $200–$750—are usually offering little more than colored water that wears off after one winter. Sealing is a maintenance tool, not a cure-all. Don’t skip it on a sound driveway, but don’t expect it to fix neglect or poor construction underneath.

Base preparation: the part you pay for and never see

What actually makes or breaks a driveway in Upper Allen isn’t how smooth the asphalt looks on day one. It’s the unseen structure underneath: the subgrade, the compacted aggregate base, and how each layer is built and packed. First, the subgrade—this is the native soil your property sits on. Around Upper Allen and much of Cumberland County, that often means limestone karst. This geology drains well, but it is prone to sinkholes and subsidence if you get careless with compaction or skimp on stone depth. The next layer is the compacted aggregate base—usually crushed limestone from local quarries. Here lies the most common shortcut: some crews will thin out the base, skip compaction between lifts, or lay new asphalt directly over old, failing materials. All of these mistakes are invisible once asphalt is down. Over the base goes the asphalt, laid and rolled hot. But no matter how thick or pretty the top layer looks, if it sits on a thin, soft, or loose base, repeated freeze-thaw cycles in our climate will destroy it from underneath. Frost heave isn’t a myth: water in the soil freezes at 30 inches deep in Upper Allen, expands, and lifts or breaks the driveway slab. When spring comes, the thawed ground settles unevenly, cracking your investment. Cheap quotes are usually cheap for a reason: less excavation, less stone, skipped grading. You won’t see any of it until year three, when the driveway settles or cracks. Here’s what you should see in writing before saying yes to any contractor:
  • Base depth: 6–8 inches of compacted aggregate for asphalt, at least 4 inches for concrete.
  • Aggregate type: local crushed limestone, not recycled debris.
  • Compaction: must be done in layers (“lifts”), with proof of compaction (roller or vibratory plate on each lift).
The upfront investment in a properly built base pays itself back every winter in Upper Allen—when shortcuts get exposed, and repairs cost thousands more than doing it right the first time.

What you need before paving in Upper Allen

In Upper Allen, repaving or resurfacing an existing asphalt driveway usually does not require a building permit, provided you are not expanding the surface area. This means if you are simply restoring what’s already there, you can skip the trip to the township office. However, where your driveway meets the public road—the approach—permitting is a different matter. For driveways entering a state route (such as those near US-15 or PA-114), a Highway Occupancy Permit is issued by PennDOT District 8-0, not by the township. If your driveway connects to a township road, you’ll need to check with Upper Allen Township for their permit requirements. Skipping this can mean trouble later, especially if you plan to sell your home. Expanding your driveway or adding new impervious surface can bring stormwater management into play. Local rules may require site grading or runoff management, and directing water onto a neighbor’s property can result in legal liability—regardless of whether you pulled a permit. Never sign with a paving contractor in Pennsylvania unless you first verify their Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration—look it up at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. For businesses with employees, workers’ compensation coverage is mandatory and can be confirmed quickly via WCAIS at wcais.pa.gov. These are state-level checks and apply to all jobs over $500. A written contract is legally required for any driveway work over $500, and you always have three business days to cancel under Pennsylvania law. Don’t let any contractor tell you otherwise.

Upper Allen's ground and what it means for paving

Upper Allen and much of Cumberland County sit on limestone karst, a geological formation that drains well but brings unique risks for paving. Karst means the underlying soil can shift or settle unpredictably, creating the sinkhole issues sometimes seen around Mechanicsburg and Carlisle. For a driveway, this means you cannot cut corners on base thickness or compaction—without a solid, uniformly compacted stone base, the whole structure can settle or fracture, sometimes unevenly. Combine the karst base with local frost depth, which is 30 inches here, and you have a recipe for powerful freeze-thaw movement every winter. What this means for your project is that driveways built during the post-1960s suburban boom (think the wide driveways in neighborhoods strung along the I-81 corridor) may have only received nominal base prep. Many are now showing their age, making overlays or even full dig-out and replacement more common. Elsewhere in Pennsylvania, you might be able to get away with a thinner base or cheaper aggregate. Around Upper Allen, with its settling risk, a proper base is non-negotiable. If you’re repairing or replacing a driveway that’s older than 30 years, assume base standards were lower back then and plan for correction. Smart contractors here will walk your project area looking for signs of past settlement and will recommend thicker aggregate to make up for weaker spots in the ground. Even if you don’t see a sinkhole, the principle holds: with limestone karst, do it right or do it twice.

The driveway scams that work in Upper Allen

Watch for these pitches: someone knocks, claiming they have “leftover asphalt from a job down the street” and can offer you a deal if you decide today. Sometimes, they leave you with a verbal estimate but no written contract or receipts. Other times it’s a sealcoating scam: a thin coat of dilute oil, sprayed in ten minutes for less than $200–$750, gone in the next good rain. The protections exist for a reason. In Pennsylvania, you are entitled to a written contract for any driveway work over $500 (the threshold for virtually all driveways), and you have a three-business-day right of cancellation by law. Don’t ever let anyone pressure you out of these rights. It’s illegal for contractors to waive your insurance deductible, and you can check any Home Improvement Contractor’s (HIC) registration instantly at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. In five minutes, you can check the contractor’s HIC number, confirm workers’ compensation coverage through WCAIS, and get your contract in writing with the full job scope—base thickness, material, drainage plan, and warranty. Never pay cash up front and don’t sign away your rights. These steps defeat every driveway scam that works in Upper Allen.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Upper Allen

Why are paving quotes in Upper Allen so different from each other? Most of the price difference you see between driveway quotes in Upper Allen comes from things you’ll never see when the job is done. The big variable is always base preparation: how much of the old material is excavated, how deep the new stone base goes, how well it’s compacted, and whether proper grading and drainage are done. Skipping these steps makes a quote much cheaper on paper, but the surface often fails in a few years due to our region’s freeze-thaw cycles and the limestone karst subgrade. Another factor is insurance, licensing, and actual written scope. Low quotes rarely include a written warranty or proper compaction and thickness. You may get prices ranging from $1,550–$9,400 for a “driveway replacement” here, but the difference is usually all underground. The best value isn’t always the lowest price, but the quote that shows all layers, thicknesses, and guarantees in writing.
What is the difference between crack filling and sealcoating? Crack filling and sealcoating address very different issues with your driveway. Crack filling is about sealing individual gaps—usually less than a quarter inch wide—before water can penetrate and reach the stone base below. This prevents the freeze-thaw cycles, common in Upper Allen, from enlarging cracks and breaking up the base. Sealcoating, on the other hand, applies a thin protective coating over the entire asphalt surface. It blocks UV rays, oil, and water, and rejuvenates the driveway’s color, but it doesn’t address structural problems or fill deep cracks. If you skip crack filling, water gets in and causes expensive long-term damage, regardless of how often you sealcoat. Good crack filling ($200–$800) actually extends driveway life here; sealcoating alone is mainly cosmetic unless combined with spot repairs.
Are pavers worth the cost in south-central Pennsylvania? For most Upper Allen homeowners, concrete or asphalt will deliver the best value for the climate and ground conditions here. Pavers do offer a high-end look and excellent drainage, but they carry a hefty price—often double or more the cost of a standard concrete drive ($5,200–$12,500). With our freeze-thaw cycles and the limestone karst subgrade in Cumberland County, pavers must be set over a properly designed, deep base to avoid shifting or settling. If done right, paver driveways can last as long as poured concrete, but repairs are much more labor-intensive if sections settle unevenly. Unless you’re looking for a custom appearance and are prepared to maintain and possibly re-level pavers every few years, you’ll get more durability for less cost from concrete or high-grade asphalt in this area.
Do I need an approach permit where my driveway meets the road? Yes, in most cases you will need an approach permit for the section where your driveway meets a public road in Upper Allen. What surprises many is that for driveways connecting to a state route, the permit is not from the township but from PennDOT District 8-0, based on the Highway Occupancy Permit process. For township roads, you’ll work with the township office. Failing to pull this permit—especially if changing or enlarging the approach—can cause problems with property resale or even require expensive corrections. The requirement applies whether you are installing a new surface or simply replacing what’s already there. Permit costs are modest compared to the overall price of a new asphalt drive ($3,700–$9,400), and getting this step right keeps your improvements legal and straightforward.
What causes concrete to spall or flake in Upper Allen? Spalling in concrete driveways in Upper Allen is almost always the result of freeze-thaw cycles, often made worse by using de-icing salts. When salty water soaks into concrete and then freezes, the surface layers expand and break away—leaving rough, pitted patches. Our winter design temperature of 12 °F and frequent fluctuations above and below freezing make these cycles common. Poor finishing techniques during installation (such as overworking or adding too much water to the surface) can make it worse. Maintenance matters: never put salt or chemical de-icers on fresh concrete in the first winter. When resurfacing or replacing, proper base and water drainage are the best preventions. A well-installed concrete drive in Upper Allen can last 30–50 years ($5,200–$12,500) if you avoid early pitfall mistakes.
How far ahead should I book a paver in Upper Allen? Spring and early summer are the busiest times for paving contractors in Upper Allen. For the best schedule and project planning, you should call and get on the calendar at least four to eight weeks ahead for asphalt or concrete driveways, especially for full replacements ($3,700–$9,400). After a harsh winter, backlogs can run deeper if there have been widespread freeze-thaw issues. Most quality outfits are booked out for months during the peak, so starting the process in late winter or very early spring is smart. If someone offers to start tomorrow, especially as a cold call, be suspicious—it may be a sign of a transient outfit rather than a locally established company. Schedule early for the best results, and it gives you time to check HIC registration and crew credentials.
What kind of soil is under Upper Allen driveways? Driveways in Upper Allen typically sit on limestone karst subgrade, which sets our area apart from many other parts of south-central Pennsylvania. This soil type drains well but can settle unpredictably, requiring a thicker and more carefully compacted aggregate base than you’d use in clay-heavy regions. Karst can also form hidden voids, so proper compaction and base depth are non-negotiable here. If your driveway was replaced or built before the 1980s, original standards may have been looser—modern replacements ($3,700–$9,400 for asphalt) should always include upgraded base work. If you’re seeing unexplained settling or new dips, it’s often the ground, not just the pavement, that’s the culprit.
What does compaction in lifts mean? "Compaction in lifts" is the process of building up the driveway’s gravel base in thin layers—usually 2–4 inches at a time—and then compacting each layer before the next is added. This is essential in Upper Allen due to our limestone karst subgrade and pronounced freeze-thaw cycles. Laying all the stone at once and trying to pack it down will leave soft spots or voids underneath the asphalt or concrete, even if it looks smooth on top. These weak points later allow settling, cracking, or full collapse—sometimes years later. Reputable pavers state their lift thickness and compaction method in writing on contracts for full replacements ($3,700–$9,400). If a contractor skips this step to save time, you’ll pay for it tenfold in repairs or premature failure.
How much does a concrete driveway cost in Upper Allen? For a properly installed concrete driveway in Upper Allen, you’re looking at a price range of $5,200–$12,500, with a typical job for a two-car suburban drive landing around $8,400. The main drivers of cost are square footage and how much base prep is needed, especially given our limestone karst conditions. Going for a thicker base (which is smart here due to settling risk) and quality finishing work can move the cost to the higher end. Concrete lasts decades—good install can mean 30–50 years without major work—but it’s not immune to spalling if exposed to harsh de-icers or improper drainage. Always demand full details in writing before signing, including base depth, reinforcement, control joints, and water management.
