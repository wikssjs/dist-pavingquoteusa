Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors who perform covered home improvement work above $500 are generally required to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before you sign, verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be especially cautious of door-to-door offers after storms (“storm chasers”)—a contractor without verifiable PA HIC registration is a serious red flag.

Permits: for residential driveway paving, a building permit is often not required for the paving work itself in many Pennsylvania municipalities, but the driveway approach where it meets the public road may require a driveway access permit (or municipal approval) before excavation. Check with your local municipality and/or PennDOT for work involving the road right-of-way, culverts, or changes to the driveway cut.

Runoff matters under Pennsylvania stormwater rules (Chapter 102). Contractors should grade the site so the project does not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way; you may also need drainage solutions if your driveway currently channels water toward a neighbor.

Finally, verify workers’ compensation coverage at wcais.pa.gov. Uninsured crews on your property can leave you exposed to liability. To verify a contractor in Exeter, start with: (1) HIC status online; (2) certificate of insurance (general liability and, if applicable, auto) naming you/your property as required by contract; (3) workers’ comp proof via the WC registry; and (4) a clear written scope showing what they will excavate, dispose of, and pave.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your Exeter driveway?

In Exeter, Pennsylvania, the freeze–thaw cycle is the deciding factor. Both asphalt and concrete expand and contract with temperature swings, but they fail in different ways and respond differently to Pennsylvania winters and road salt. Asphalt is flexible, so it typically handles freeze–thaw movement better at the surface. The tradeoff is maintenance: homeowners usually need sealcoating every 2–3 years to protect against oxidation and water intrusion—especially after salt season.

Concrete is rigid and long-lasting, but it can spall or crack when repeated freeze–thaw events and deicers get into joints and edges. With 69.4% of Exeter homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many driveways are already at or past their original design life, and the base often needs attention. In those cases, full replacement is frequently the best value because you’re rebuilding the base and improving drainage—not just covering cracks.

Here’s a real-world style pricing example: if your driveway is settled and has widespread cracking, choosing concrete only to “save money” on base work usually costs more long-term. A properly scoped asphalt replacement commonly falls in the $4,800–$11,000 band, while concrete installation often lands in the $6,500–$15,000 range. If both options rebuild the base, asphalt may be the better value for homeowners prioritizing cost control and faster restoration during the paving season; if you want a low-maintenance surface for decades and you’re prepared for joint/spall considerations, concrete can make sense—especially on driveways that receive less direct salt splash.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in Exeter

Choosing the right contractor in Exeter is mostly about verification and scope clarity. First, confirm licensing and certifications. In Pennsylvania, paving contractors should be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) if the job exceeds $500—verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Next, if the contractor claims expertise with asphalt repairs and maintenance standards, ask about IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) credentials where applicable to the scope; use the certification registry page or provide your sales contact’s certificate details so you can confirm active status.

Then get 2–3 itemized written quotes. Avoid “per driveway” lump sums only. Request a breakdown that shows labor and materials separately—excavation depth, stone base thickness, asphalt thickness (including lifts), tack coat, disposal/hauling, and edging details. Make sure the quote states what is excluded (for example: removing trees/roots, relocating drainage, replacing subbase beyond certain limits, or repairing failing culverts).

Warranties should be in writing with two parts: workmanship warranty length and material/equipment coverage. Payment schedules matter: never pay more than 10–15% upfront; hold back until the job is complete and the final grade/finish has been accepted. Finally, ask for a timeline in writing—arrival date estimate and completion window—because peak paving season (late spring through early fall) can involve 3–8 week lead times in Luzerne County.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in Exeter?

In Exeter, sealcoating is a maintenance step that works best on asphalt that’s structurally sound—no pumping, no base failure, and no major edge collapse. Typically, most homeowners sealcoat every 2–3 years to protect against oxidation and water intrusion from Pennsylvania freeze–thaw cycles and salt. If your driveway is new asphalt, allow time for it to cure before sealcoating (your contractor should specify the waiting period based on weather and mix). Also note that sealcoating won’t stop alligator cracking or settlement caused by compromised base; those problems usually require full replacement rather than a maintenance coat. If you’re budgeting, local sealcoating pricing commonly falls in the $300–$900 band for a standard residential driveway per application, but the exact cost depends on area and prep (crack filling, cleaning, and patching). For the best results, coordinate sealcoating timing after repairs are done and when the forecast supports dry, warm conditions for curing.

What causes driveway cracks in Exeter?

Cracks in Exeter driveways usually come from water movement and pavement movement over time. The biggest contributor is the freeze–thaw cycle: water gets under asphalt or into small voids, freezes, expands, and then thaws—repeating many times through a season. That movement can open up cracks, break edges, and lead to potholes or alligator cracking when the base is weakened. In older Luzerne County housing stock, many driveways were installed decades ago; with 69.4% of Exeter homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), base layers may have settled or lost fines stability, which accelerates cracking. Poor drainage and low spots are also common—if your driveway doesn’t shed water away from the garage or toward a proper swale, cracks appear sooner. Lastly, patchwork repairs can cause “reflective” issues when the underlying base isn’t corrected.

Does homeowner's insurance cover driveway damage in Exeter?

It depends on the cause and your policy language. Most homeowner’s insurance plans do not cover long-term wear and deterioration (like cracks from freeze–thaw cycling). However, damage from a sudden, covered event—such as a pipe burst that floods the driveway, a fallen tree during a storm, or certain kinds of accidental damage—may be considered. If you’re working through a claim, it’s important that the contractor documents existing conditions with photos, measurements, and a written scope showing what was repaired or replaced and why. After a covered event, you may see water-related base issues that later turn into potholes; in those cases, repair timing matters. For coverage questions, contact your insurer directly and keep receipts and written contractor documentation.

How thick should asphalt be for a PA residential driveway?

Asphalt thickness is tied to the base condition, driveway use, and whether you’re installing new asphalt or resurfacing. For standard residential installations in Pennsylvania, reputable contractors commonly build a compacted stone base (often 4–6 inches for typical sites) and place asphalt in two lifts totaling about 2.5–3 inches. If you’re overlaying, the total thickness may be thinner (commonly a 2-inch overlay), but only when the existing base is structurally sound and drainage is corrected. If the driveway has settlement, broken edges, or cracking that indicates base compromise, the project should shift to full replacement rather than trying to “cap” the problem with thin asphalt. That’s why two bids for the same driveway can differ significantly in price—base preparation depth and asphalt thickness affect the materials and labor. Homeowners in Exeter often see full replacement estimates landing in the $4,800–$11,000 range, while resurfacing/overlay may fall around $2,100–$6,300 when base conditions allow it.
