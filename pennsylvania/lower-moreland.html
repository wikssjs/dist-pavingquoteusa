Permits and approvals for paving in Lower Moreland

In Lower Moreland, you’re generally not required to pull a municipal permit if you’re simply resurfacing or sealcoating a driveway in its existing footprint. Routine sealcoating, for example, can usually proceed right away without doing paperwork (FACTS). However, things change when your project involves the driveway approach—the section where your driveway meets a public road. For approach work, permits are managed not by your local code office, but typically by the Montgomery Township Public Works Department or the township-specific authority responsible for road occupancy (FACTS). This is a separate process, and it can catch homeowners off guard.

If your project expands the driveway or increases impervious area, runoff and stormwater management come into play. Montgomery County’s moderate freeze-thaw cycles mean that drainage is critical: excess water can degrade your driveway and creates liability if runoff is directed onto a neighbor’s property, regardless of what the permit says.

Any contract over $500 with a paving contractor must be in writing under the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, and you have a three-day right to cancel. Always verify contractor registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov, and check that they carry mandatory workers’ compensation coverage using the WCAIS system (wcais.pa.gov). This is your protection if anyone is injured on your property during the work.

Asphalt or concrete for a Lower Moreland driveway?

Lower Moreland, like much of southeastern Pennsylvania, sits in a climate with moderate freeze-thaw cycles and humid summers. Asphalt is usually the better performer here because it is flexible enough to handle the inevitable ground movement as glacial till and clay soils expand and contract. Freeze-thaw cycles cause the ground to shift, and asphalt’s flexibility allows it to bend rather than crack.

Concrete, while more durable on paper, is a rigid material. It does best in stable ground and dry, moderate climates. In practice, most failures here are from de-icing salts and winter scaling, not long cracks. If you don’t avoid salt, concrete can start to pit and flake within a few years—especially in neighborhoods where heavy salting is standard. Most owners do not, in reality, restrict de-icer usage as strictly as concrete needs. If you want low maintenance and longevity, concrete delivers, but it costs more up front and demands you avoid salt or use only calcium chloride, which is pricier than rock salt.

Paver driveways are an option for aesthetics or if you expect to do frequent repairs—they settle with the base and let you replace units. Gravel is the budget option or for rural runs, but needs grading and top-dressing every couple of years.

Caring for a new Lower Moreland driveway

What keeps a Lower Moreland driveway intact? First is timely crack sealing—once water finds its way into the base, even a small crack, freeze-thaw cycles and silty clay soils will turn minor flaws into major repairs. Keeping driveway edges supported is next in line. Edges are the weakest spot, especially when the underlying base is thin. Avoid parking heavy vehicles or placing dumpsters near the edge, and don’t let snow banks pile up off the side for long periods.

Drainage matters as much as construction: water should always have a route away from driveway and house. The local soils can hold onto moisture, so if downspouts let out near your drive, pipe them away or expect rutting and heaving.

With new asphalt, wait at least a week before parking, and avoid stationary turning of wheels during that time—it scuffs soft pavement. Longer cure times during humid, cool weather are normal here.

On de-icing: never use regular salt on concrete—stick to calcium chloride or sand, but be prepared to pay more. On asphalt, the freeze-refreeze cycle is the greatest threat, so only melt enough ice to get traction. Heated mats are costly and rare, but a bag of calcium chloride is cheaper than a full replacement.

Choosing a paving company

Vetting a driveway contractor in Lower Moreland is more than just looking for a truck and a sign. Registration as a Home Improvement Contractor is required for projects over $500 (verify at hic.attorneygeneral.gov). Workers’ compensation insurance, even for a small crew, is mandatory and can be checked at WCAIS (wcais.pa.gov).

Look for a contractor with a local business address (not just a cell phone), who owns their own paving equipment. Montgomery County’s aggregate and asphalt are supplied from local plants along the I-276 and US 202 corridors (FACTS)—ask which plant supplies their mix. If they can’t answer, they may be brokering or renting a crew, and you could be left holding the bag.

Beyond replacement: layout changes

If you plan to widen your driveway for a second car, run it out to a detached garage, or convert a gravel lane to asphalt, there’s more to consider than just the topping. Any change to the driveway’s layout—new width, length, or a parking pad—means disturbing new soil, so the base must be rebuilt from scratch, not just tied into the old. The joint where old and new meet is always a permanent weak spot, so expect a higher risk of future cracking there.

Adding impervious surface (concrete or asphalt) might push you into needing a stormwater management review, since runoff has to stay on your property. Any expansion or new approach to the road likely means you’ll need a permit from Montgomery Township Public Works or your township authority before beginning. These requirements catch a lot of homeowners off guard—don’t pour until you’re sure.

Also, be aware of local setback rules that may limit how close you can pave to a property line, which matter in Lower Moreland’s suburban lots. If you’re replacing gravel with asphalt, the cost shifts higher, but you’ll gain a smoother, cleaner finish that’s easier to maintain through southeastern Pennsylvania’s humid summers and freeze-thaw winters.

Overlay, patch or full replacement?

The real decision for a Lower Moreland driveway comes down to the condition of what’s underneath—not what’s visible on top. Here’s how to spot the difference yourself before taking a contractor’s word for it.

Alligator cracking looks like scales, running across a wide stretch; it nearly always means the base has failed and any overlay is money burned. Isolated linear cracks—especially under a quarter inch—tell you the surface is just aging but the structure is sound. Birdbaths or depressions show the subgrade is shifting, which calls for digging out and rebuilding that spot. Crumbling at the edges points to inadequate edge support, which a patch won’t address unless it’s rebuilt. Potholes mean water has reached the base: patch the hole, but plan for further work unless you fix drainage.

Be wary of any low-priced quote for an overlay that doesn’t probe or core the driveway first. If the base is gone, an overlay will be ruined by next spring.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in Lower Moreland

How much does a concrete driveway cost in Lower Moreland? A concrete driveway in Lower Moreland typically runs $5,900–$14,000 for a new installation. This covers a standard suburban single-family home, which makes up almost 85% of Lower Moreland’s housing stock (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024). The final price depends on square footage, removal of the existing surface, and the thickness of both concrete and base, especially due to the area’s clay-rich subgrade. Remember, concrete is a durable but up-front investment, and long-term success depends on surface prep and controlling drainage. Skip salt-based de-icers and ask your contractor whether they’ll dowel joints for better longevity in this climate.
Why are paving quotes in Lower Moreland so different from each other? Price differences on paving bids here almost always come from what’s happening below the surface. Lower Moreland’s glacial till and silty clay soils require a solid base, and that’s where costs add up. Some contractors quote only thin resurfacing, while others price for a complete rebuild. A basic asphalt overlay runs $1,750–$5,300, but a full-depth replacement is $4,100–$10,500. Ask what they plan to do with the base and how they manage drainage—these are the real separators. Cheap is expensive if the subgrade isn’t fixed, and price should reflect actual labor and material, not shortcuts.
What slope does a driveway need for drainage? For Lower Moreland, a minimum slope of 2% (about 1/4 inch per foot) is recommended for new driveways. That means water will run off efficiently even after a heavy summer storm or a winter thaw. With the silty clay soils local to this part of Montgomery County, it’s even more important to avoid flat spots, since the ground won’t drain easily. If your drive is being replaced, make sure the contractor discusses where the water will go—it’s worth spending on grading versus chasing muddy edges later. Typical grading is included in a full rebuild, which starts at $4,100–$10,500.
What kind of soil is under Lower Moreland driveways? Most Lower Moreland driveways sit atop a mix of glacial till and silty clay subgrade (FACTS). This soil is prone to holding moisture and expanding during freeze-thaw cycles, which is why driveways here need a thicker gravel or stone base than you’ll see west of the river. If your existing drive is rutting or has birdbaths, soil conditions may be the culprit. This means base work, not just an overlay, which is priced in the $4,100–$10,500 range for asphalt.
Can I pave over my existing driveway? Sometimes, yes—and sometimes it’s a waste of money. In Lower Moreland, if your existing base is strong and surface cracks are minor, an asphalt overlay can add 8–15 years and typically costs $1,750–$5,300. But if you have alligator cracking, deep depressions, or lots of edge crumbling, the base has failed and anything placed over it will not last. Ask your contractor to core or probe before quoting. Don’t let anyone just “blacktop over” serious problems.
When can I seal a brand new asphalt driveway? In Lower Moreland’s climate, you’ll want to wait about 90 days after a new asphalt driveway is installed before applying sealcoat. This allows time for the asphalt to properly cure, especially during our humid and sometimes rainy summers. If you seal too soon, the surface can trap oils, leaving it soft and prone to marking. A single application of sealcoat runs $225–$800 and can extend driveway life—but only after the pavement has set up well. If in doubt, ask your paving contractor for timing specific to your project.
What causes concrete to spall or flake in Lower Moreland? The leading cause is winter de-icing salts combined with the region’s freeze-thaw cycles. When salt is used on concrete, especially in a climate like Lower Moreland, it penetrates the surface and chemically attacks the paste, causing pitting and eventual flaking (spalling). Our local builders see it more on driveways where regular rock salt is used. The best prevention is using calcium chloride or sand instead, which costs more up front but saves on replacement. Even with perfect pours, concrete driveways here reach the upper end of their 30–50 year lifespan only if owners are strict about winter care. A new driveway runs $5,900–$14,000, so treating it with care pays off.
Can asphalt be laid in cold weather? Asphalt can’t be installed when the ground or air temperature is below about 45°F. For Lower Moreland, paving season is roughly April to early November. Laying hot mix in colder weather means it won’t compact properly, leaving voids and premature cracks. If you’re offered a late-season deal, be wary—it often doesn’t pay off. If you have to patch or keep something functional, cold patch is available but isn’t a permanent solution. Expect full-depth repairs or overlays to be priced in the $1,750–$10,500 range depending on scope.
Do I need a permit to repave a driveway in Lower Moreland? Generally, you don’t need a permit if you’re resurfacing or sealcoating your existing driveway’s footprint in Lower Moreland. However, if you’re changing the layout, widening, or working on the part near the street (driveway approach), you’ll likely need an occupancy or street opening permit from Montgomery Township Public Works or your township’s own department. It’s always worth confirming before you start, especially for bigger projects. Permit fees and requirements are not included in your paving contract, but a routine sealcoat (usually $225–$800) proceeds without that red tape.
