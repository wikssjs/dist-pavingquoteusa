Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, paving contractors performing work above certain thresholds must be properly registered. If the job exceeds $500, contractors typically need to be registered as Home Improvement Contractors (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. You can verify a contractor at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Be especially cautious if a contractor is soliciting driveway work door-to-door after storms; “storm chasers” without verifiable PA HIC registration are a significant red flag.

Permits vary by municipality and by where the driveway connects. Residential driveway paving often does not require a building permit in most Pennsylvania municipalities, but the driveway approach (where your driveway meets a public road) may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or your local municipality—particularly if work changes the slope, touches a right-of-way, or affects existing drainage. It’s smart to check before excavation so you don’t end up redoing work or delaying the project.

Water runoff matters in Pennsylvania. Under stormwater requirements (commonly referenced as Chapter 102), paving projects should not increase runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. Contractors should plan proper grading and, if needed, drainage solutions.

Finally, verify workers’ compensation coverage on wcais.pa.gov. If a crew is uninsured, you could be exposed to liability if someone is injured on your property.

Step-by-step for West Mifflin homeowners: (1) confirm HIC registration online; (2) request an up-to-date certificate of insurance (general liability and auto, if equipment will be driven on-site); (3) verify workers’ compensation status; (4) ask which permits apply to your driveway approach and who will handle the paperwork; (5) keep copies of everything in writing with your estimate.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your West Mifflin driveway?

In West Mifflin, Pennsylvania, the deciding factor between asphalt and concrete is the region’s freeze–thaw cycle and how your driveway handles water and salt exposure. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they tend to fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, which often helps it resist cracking from freeze–thaw stress; however, it needs routine surface care—commonly sealcoating every 2–3 years—to protect the binder and slow surface oxidation. Concrete is rigid and can last a long time, but in Allegheny County it’s more sensitive to spalling or cracking when water and road salt get into joints and the freeze–thaw repeats through the season.

With 87.4% of West Mifflin homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), many driveways are at or beyond expected service life, and full replacement is a common decision rather than a long-term resurfacing plan. Asphalt often gets the nod when homeowners want a forgiving surface that’s easier to maintain and can better tolerate minor movement—especially when the base is built correctly. Concrete can be a strong fit when you’re planning a long-horizon investment and want low routine maintenance, as long as the installation includes a well-prepared base, correct thickness, proper jointing, and realistic drainage management.

To ground the choice in dollars: a typical asphalt full replacement might land in the $4,500–$11,000 range, while concrete new installation commonly runs $7,000–$16,000. That difference can be justified when you have a stable site, you want decades of service, and you’re willing to invest up front for long-term performance. If the driveway base is failing, however, switching materials won’t “fix” structural issues—base and drainage still drive the outcome.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in West Mifflin

Choosing the right contractor matters as much as the material. Start by verifying credentials the easy way. In Pennsylvania, check HIC registration through the PA Attorney General’s Office at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Also ask about IICRC-style credentials and paving/trade certifications relevant to asphalt/concrete surface work—then confirm licensing and standing through official registries when applicable. For liability and safety, verify workers’ compensation at wcais.pa.gov and request your contractor’s certificates of insurance before work begins.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. You want a breakdown that clarifies excavation/removal, base material and compaction depth, asphalt or concrete thickness, edging, drainage/grading, and hauling/disposal. Avoid one-page “lump sum” pricing that doesn’t show what’s included—on older Allegheny County driveways, missing base prep scope is the common reason bids don’t match.

Read the scope carefully for exclusions: will they regrade to fix ponding, rebuild failed edges, sawcut and properly joint concrete, and handle any existing utility cuts? Confirm whether documentation for insurance claims (if you’re using coverage) is provided. Ask for a clear warranty: workmanship length, and separate coverage for equipment/materials (where offered). For payment, never pay more than 10–15% upfront; hold a portion until the job is complete and cleanup is verified. Finally, request an estimated arrival date and completion window in writing—reputable contractors in the Pittsburgh area often book 4–8 weeks out in peak season.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in West Mifflin?

Most homeowners in West Mifflin sealcoat asphalt once the new pavement has had time to cure and then recoat on a preventive schedule. A common target is sealcoating every 2–3 years depending on sun exposure, vehicle traffic, and how the surface handles salt and winter wetness. Sealcoating is best when the asphalt is structurally sound—tight surface cracks, no major rutting, and no evidence the base is failing.

If you’re evaluating timing, a good contractor will check crack severity and look for signs of movement. If the driveway has widespread fatigue cracking or base-related settlement, sealcoating won’t fix the cause and can hide issues until they worsen. In that case, resurfacing or replacement will usually be more cost-effective. Sealcoating for a standard driveway commonly falls in the $250–$800 range per application.

Since West Mifflin sees repeated freeze–thaw cycles, applying sealcoat before heavy winter seasons can help protect the asphalt’s surface chemistry. Your paving contractor should recommend an installation window that fits current weather and temperature conditions.

What causes driveway cracks in West Mifflin?

Driveway cracks in West Mifflin usually come from a combination of age, water, and winter cycles. The most common triggers are: (1) water getting into joints and the asphalt/base interface, then expanding during freeze–thaw; (2) base settlement or poor compaction, which creates stress points under tires; (3) inadequate thickness for the local climate and traffic; and (4) edge failure where water escapes under the pavement and undermines the perimeter.

Because many West Mifflin homes are older—87.4% built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—driveways have often already been through multiple cycles of snow, thawing, and salt exposure, plus occasional utility cuts. That’s why we often see alligator cracking and seam cracking that correlates with drainage problems or patchwork areas.

Small, isolated cracks may be addressed with crack filling and patching. But if cracks are widespread or you see rutting/ponding, you’re typically looking at the need for resurfacing or full replacement—because freeze–thaw damage will continue until the base and drainage are corrected.

It depends on the cause of the damage and the specific terms of your policy. Because freeze–thaw cycling and winter salt commonly worsen existing pavement conditions in West Mifflin, insurers may view long-term deterioration as maintenance-related unless there was a sudden triggering event.
