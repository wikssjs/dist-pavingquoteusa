Permits & regulations in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, if a driveway paving job exceeds $500, the paving contractor generally must be registered as a Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) through the PA Attorney General’s Office. Before anyone starts, verify registration at hic.attorneygeneral.gov. After storms, “storm chasers” sometimes show up with aggressive offers—if you can’t verify a real HIC record, treat it as a major red flag.

Permits: many residential driveway paving projects typically do not require a building permit in most PA municipalities, especially for work entirely on private property. However, the driveway approach—where the driveway meets the public road—may require a driveway access permit from PennDOT or the local municipality. Because your apron, throat, or curb area can affect public infrastructure, you should confirm this before excavation.

Water runoff: Pennsylvania stormwater requirements under Chapter 102 aim to prevent paving projects from increasing runoff onto adjacent properties or public rights-of-way. Contractors should handle grading properly and may need drainage solutions rather than “hoping” water sheds away.

Workers’ compensation: confirm coverage at wcais.pa.gov. If a crew is uninsured, you can become responsible for injuries or related costs on your property.

Step-by-step for a New Hanover homeowner: (1) check the HIC record online; (2) request a Certificate of Insurance naming you as additional insured if your municipality/custom contract requires it; (3) provide proof of workers’ compensation from wcais.pa.gov; and (4) ask whether any PennDOT/local driveway access permit applies based on your driveway’s location at the road.

Asphalt vs. concrete — what's right for your New Hanover driveway?

For New Hanover homeowners, Pennsylvania’s freeze–thaw cycle is the deciding factor in choosing asphalt versus concrete. Both materials expand and contract with temperature swings, but they tend to fail differently. Asphalt is flexible, which helps it tolerate freeze–thaw movement without immediately cracking open like rigid pavement. That flexibility works well when the pavement still has structural integrity—meaning the base isn’t failing and water isn’t repeatedly saturating the layers.

Concrete, on the other hand, is rigid. It can be very durable when properly installed, but it’s more prone to issues like spalling or cracking under heavy freeze–thaw cycling and road salt exposure. In Montgomery County, that combination shows up often enough that many older driveways eventually need more than a patch. With about 32.2% of local homes built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024), it’s common to see original driveways reach or exceed their intended service life—then overlays become less realistic and full replacement becomes the smarter move.

Where the choice gets practical is in maintenance planning. Asphalt typically needs sealcoating every 2–3 years when the surface is tight and the base is sound; it’s protective and cosmetic, not a structural fix. Concrete tends to require less routine maintenance, but repairs can be more visible and may be more disruptive when slabs need attention.

Concrete vs asphalt price-wise: if your driveway is a straightforward replacement, asphalt often lands in the $4,500–$16,000 range, while new concrete typically falls around $6,000–$18,000. That extra upfront cost is usually justified when you want the lowest day-to-day maintenance and you’re comfortable managing drainage and deicer practices to protect the surface in winter.

How to choose a driveway paving contractor in New Hanover

Choosing the right contractor in New Hanover starts with credentials you can verify. First, check IICRC certification (where applicable) and confirm any required Pennsylvania licensing/registration. For Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) status, use hic.attorneygeneral.gov. Second, verify workers’ compensation coverage through wcais.pa.gov. Third, request a current Certificate of Insurance—general liability is standard, and additional insured status may be required by your township or by your contract.

Next, get 2–3 itemized written quotes. Avoid “lump sum only” offers. Ask for a breakdown that shows labor and materials line-by-line (excavation, base stone and compaction, asphalt mix and thickness/lifts, trucking/hauling, and finishing). If there’s an asphalt overlay, ask whether they will mill, prime (if used), and how thickness will be matched to the existing driveway. Also request documentation on any warranty exclusions—especially around drainage issues, settlement, or soil conditions.

Warranty: confirm workmanship warranty length and whether materials have a manufacturer warranty (and what it actually covers). Payment schedules should be conservative: never pay more than 10–15% upfront, and hold back until the job is complete and clean-up is finished. Finally, get a written arrival estimate and completion window, because Montgomery County leads can run 3–6 weeks during peak season for larger work.

Frequently asked questions — driveway paving in New Hanover

What are the signs of a failing driveway base in New Hanover?

If the base is failing in New Hanover, the driveway usually shows “system” problems—issues that resurfacing alone can’t fix. Look for heaving or settlement spots that don’t level off after rain, repeated cracking patterns (including alligator cracking) that return quickly, and areas where the pavement looks pushed up or broken into small irregular pieces. Another common sign is edge failure: asphalt starts pulling away near the garage apron or along the driveway edges because water is migrating into the base. In Montgomery County’s freeze–thaw conditions, ponding is also a clue—if you consistently see damp soil or water standing after storms, the base can be saturated and then freeze. Finally, if you’ve recently had sealcoating or crack filling and the same cracks reappear within a short window, that often points to structural movement below the surface.

When the base is failing, full replacement with reconstructed base is usually the right fix; in 2026 terms, that’s commonly aligned with the $4,500–$16,000 driveway replacement band rather than just a surface overlay.

How much does driveway paving cost in New Hanover, PA?

Driveway paving cost in New Hanover depends most on square footage, how much base work is needed, and how much water/drainage correction is required. For many Pennsylvania homeowners, quotes vary because contractors may recommend different approaches: overlay versus full reconstruction. If your driveway’s base is structurally sound and you mainly have surface cracking, resurfacing commonly falls in the $2,400–$9,000 range in 2026 estimates. If excavation, base rebuild (often 6–10 inches), and new asphalt are required due to failed base or major cracking, full replacement commonly aligns with the $4,500–$16,000 band.

Sealcoating is another pricing piece homeowners sometimes underestimate. It’s typically preventive—when the asphalt is still tight and structurally intact—and is often in the $250–$900 range per application for a standard driveway. Concrete is generally higher upfront in this market: new concrete installations often land around $6,000–$18,000.

How long does a new asphalt driveway last in New Hanover's climate?

A new asphalt driveway in New Hanover commonly lasts about 20–30 years when it’s built on a properly compacted base, with adequate asphalt thickness and correct drainage. The climate matters because Montgomery County’s freeze–thaw cycle can accelerate deterioration if water infiltrates layers or if the driveway settles. That’s why we emphasize base preparation: if the base is undersized, contaminated, or poorly compacted, the pavement will shift and crack over time—even if it looks good at first.

Maintenance also plays a big role. If your asphalt still has structural integrity (tight surface, limited cracking, and no major heaving/alligator failure), sealcoating every 2–3 years helps protect the surface binder from weathering and oxidation. In budgeting terms, homeowners often plan for $250–$900 per application depending on driveway size and condition. If you wait until the pavement is structurally failing, sealcoating can’t restore what’s broken underneath.

Should I repair or replace my driveway in New Hanover?

Repair is usually the better choice when damage is minor and localized—such as small cracks under about 1/4 inch, a few shallow spots, or isolated patch areas on a driveway that’s otherwise flat and stable. Crack filling and patching only are typically a short-term fix, often aligning with the $200–$800 range per application in 2026 estimates and lasting roughly 1–3 years depending on conditions.

Replacement or full reconstruction is more likely when you see widespread cracking (including alligator cracking), heaving, failed edges, or settlement that indicates base failure. In those cases, resurfacing/overlay may “trap” the problem and shorten lifespan. For homeowners whose driveways have reached the end of their service life—common with older housing stock, where about 32.2% of homes were built before 1980 (U.S. Census Bureau, ACS 2024)—replacement tends to be the most realistic long-term solution.

A practical rule: if the problem is structural movement or drainage-related saturation, replacement beats repeated patching.

How do I find a reputable paving contractor in New Hanover, PA?

Also ask for proof of workers’ compensation through wcais.pa.gov, and request a current Certificate of Insurance.

When should I sealcoat my asphalt driveway in New Hanover?

Sealcoating is best thought of as protective maintenance—not a fix for structural failure. For New Hanover, that means sealcoat when the asphalt surface is still tight, the driveway is mostly stable, and cracks are manageable. A common schedule is every 2–3 years, especially if you’re seeing oxidation, slight fading, or hairline cracking that hasn’t turned into wide, moving breaks.

Timing matters too. Sealcoat should be applied when temperatures are suitable and the driveway is dry; applying too early or over moisture can lead to blotchy or peeling areas. Most importantly, only sealcoat if the base and pavement integrity are sound. If you have widespread alligator cracking, heaving, or evidence that water is undermining the base, sealcoating may only coat the symptoms and won’t stop failure below. In those cases, homeowners typically need resurfacing/overlay or full replacement instead—costs commonly fall in the $2,400–$9,000 overlay band or the $4,500–$16,000 replacement band depending on the base.
